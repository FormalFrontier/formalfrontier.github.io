• Finite-support product homomorphisms.   • Finite-coordinate factorization.   • Real seminormed spaces and no small subgroups. A real seminormed space's additive group has no small subgroups if and only if its seminorm topology is Hausdorff. Zero-sum pairs and multiplicative images

In a nonunital, nonassociative semiring, a + b = 0 implies a * a = b * b. A multiplication-preserving function into any monoid sends a and b to units simultaneously, without preserving addition, zero or one or requiring commutativity of the target. See the zero-sum API.

Reduced powers and nilpotence

  • Cyclic products in a monoid with zero satisfy nilpotencyClass (a * b) ≤ nilpotencyClass (b * a) + 1, and symmetrically; see nilpotency classes.

In a reduced ring, not necessarily commutative, equality at two coprime natural powers determines an element; equality at two exponents is equivalent to equality at their gcd. In a reduced monoid with zero, positive powers preserve left and right annihilators. Aligned lists of positive powers preserve product-zero, not product values. See the complete statements and the reduced-ring power API. Over a commutative ring, equality of two powers modulo a radical ideal is equivalent to equality at their gcd, including zero exponents; see the radical-ideal API.

In any monoid with zero, nilpotencyClass_pow gives nilpotencyClass (a ^ n) = (nilpotencyClass a + n - 1) / n for every natural exponent, using total natural-number division at zero. Unital monoid maps preserving and reflecting zero preserve the class; injectivity is not required. See Algebra.GroupWithZero.Nilpotent. Separately, SemiconjBy a x a and nilpotence of x bound the nilpotency class of a by that of x plus one. Finite pairwise orthogonal sums over any semiring are nilpotent exactly when every supported summand is nilpotent, with an explicit class bound. For finite pairwise commuting families with f i ^ n i = 0, the sum vanishes to the power 1 + ∑ i ∈ s, (n i - 1). No commutativity or nontriviality of the semiring, positivity of the individual exponents, or finite index type is assumed. The catalogue records empty-support cases and the class-zero convention for nonnilpotent elements; these results use Ring.Nilpotent.Finset.

For a semiring algebra over a commutative semiring, a finite sum of pairwise commuting submodules has total nilpotency class at most one plus the sum of the predecessors of the summands' total classes. A finite span satisfies the corresponding generator-class bound when the generators' principal submodules pairwise commute; the generators themselves need not commute. Neither bound assumes summand or generator nilpotence, and both include empty support and trivial carriers. See the finite-submodule bounds.

Over a commutative semiring R, the finitely generated ideal criterion Ideal.FG.isNilpotent_iff_le_nilradical characterizes nilpotence by containment in the nilradical. The finite-span bound specializes to nilpotencyClass (Ideal.span (↑s : Set R)) ≤ 1 + ∑ x ∈ s, (nilpotencyClass x - 1). Nonnilpotent ideals have total class zero, so this upper bound alone is not a nilpotence criterion. See the finite-ideal API.

For commuting elements a, c in any monoid, a ∣ c implies a ^ n ∣ c ^ n for every natural n, including zero. In a monoid with zero, if a is nilpotent, the commuting multiple has nilpotency class at most that of a. In particular this bounds a product a * b when a commutes with b, and a list product when the nilpotent a occurs in the list and commutes with every entry. Other entries need not be nilpotent or commute with one another. These bounds retain the nilpotence premise: the total class is zero for nonnilpotent elements, so a class inequality alone is not a nilpotence criterion. See the commuting-divisor API, list-product API and full statements.

Finite sums and product homomorphisms

For submodules of a semiring equipped with a module structure over another semiring, Submodule.sum_eq_sup identifies finite sums with finite suprema, complementing the zero/bottom and addition/supremum identities. No scalar-tower, commutativity, or algebra hypothesis is required. See Algebra.Algebra.Operations.

Finite families of nonunital ring homomorphisms whose distinct images annihilate each other in both orders have a sum homomorphism, even for nonunital, nonassociative source and target semirings. For a finite dependent product of unital nonassociative semirings, Pi.liftRingHom instead builds a unital homomorphism into an associative semiring from nonunital component maps: their unit images must be pairwise orthogonal and sum to one. No commutativity, global centrality or nontriviality is assumed. The empty-index case is included; its cover-one premise forces the target to satisfy 0 = 1. See Ring.Hom.FiniteSum and the catalogue for application, zero-off extension and uniqueness laws, with their hypotheses.

Power restrictions and order

In any group, membership of every power in a finite family in a subgroup-like object is equivalent to membership of the gcd power; gcd one detects the element. The subgroup generated by the selected powers is the cyclic subgroup generated by that gcd power. Additive groups have the corresponding natural-multiple formulas. No commutativity or normality is needed; empty and all-zero families generate the trivial subgroup. See the subgroup-power API.

For any group element a and natural exponents m n, the intersection of Subgroup.zpowers (a ^ m) and Subgroup.zpowers (a ^ n) is Subgroup.zpowers (a ^ (Nat.lcm m n)). The additive version uses the integer-multiple subgroups of m • a and n • a. Zero exponents are included; no commutativity or order assumption is needed. See the same subgroup-power API.

For a finite family of natural exponents, intersecting the cyclic power subgroups with Subgroup.zpowers a gives the subgroup generated by the lcm power. Nonempty families need no initial cyclic factor; for an empty family the plain intersection is top. Additive groups have the corresponding natural-multiple formula. See the catalogue.

In a monoid, let a have injective left multiplication (left regularity). Equality of its powers and those of arbitrary b at a finite family of natural exponents is equivalent to equality at their gcd; a family with gcd one determines a = b. The same finite-family criterion holds separately when a has injective right multiplication (right regularity). No regularity of b or global cancellation is needed; additive versions hold in additive monoids. Empty families and zero exponents are included. See the power-kernel API.

Let f : M →* N, where N is a monoid and M has only multiplication and a chosen one (MulOne). If the range of f contains a positive power of every element of N, it detects equality of homomorphisms from N into any monoid with unique positive roots.

For finite image detection, let f : A →* M, with only MulOne A and monoids M, N. Suppose each x : M admits s : Finset ι and e : ι → ℕ with s.gcd e = 1 and x ^ (e i) ∈ Set.range f for every i ∈ s. For fixed g h : M →* N, left-regularity of every g x gives g.comp f = h.comp f ↔ g = h; a separate criterion uses right-regularity instead. In both, h is arbitrary, the four universes are independent, and s, e may depend on x. Additive peers use AddZero A, additive monoids and natural multiples. No global cancellation, commutativity, group structure, surjectivity, positivity, finite or nonempty index type, or decidable equality is assumed. Zero and repeated exponents are allowed; empty or all-zero families cannot have gcd one. The natural-set left interface is the ι = ℕ, e = id specialization; coprime and consecutive-power adapters remain available. Consecutive-power images give precomposition injectivity into left-cancellative targets. The catalogue distinguishes these fixed-pair image criteria from elementwise power kernels and injectivity on all homomorphisms into an arbitrary monoid.

More generally, a set of a monoid that contains, for each element, a finite family of its powers with gcd-one exponents detects equality of fixed multiplication-preserving maps into a monoid whenever the first map has left-regular values. Unit preservation and regularity of the second map are not required. The set criterion and additive form allow independent universes, element-dependent families and zero or repeated exponents.

For a multiplicatively Archimedean commutative monoid with a linear order, chosen 0, 0 ≤ 1 and strictly monotone multiplication by positive elements, a multiplication-closed set is bounded above exactly when all its members are at most 1, even for empty sets. See the power and order catalogue for additive restriction, pointwise comparison and multiplicative-map versions.

Group actions and presentations

For a commutative ring R, a commutative domain A with an R-algebra structure, and a monoid M acting on A by semiring endomorphisms, IsIntegral.finite_orbit proves that every element integral over R has finite orbit, provided [SMulCommClass M R A]. The three types may lie in independent universes. Scalar commutation fixes the image of R; no action on R, injective scalar map, faithful action, integrality of all of A, or group or finiteness assumption on M is required. See GroupAction.Integral and the finite-orbit API.

For arbitrary subgroups H, K of a group G, equivariant maps G/H → G/K exist exactly when H ≤ gKg⁻¹ for some g; the coset actions are isomorphic exactly when the subgroups are conjugate, not merely when maps exist both ways. Group-valued diagrams have presentations by free products modulo the normal closure of arrow relations. For a group homomorphism f : A →* B, a pushout along A → T with T trivial is B / normalClosure (range f). Objectwise-surjective compatible homomorphisms between two diagrams over the same arbitrary indexing category induce a surjection of colimits; its kernel is the normal closure of the component kernels' images. The object groups may have independent universes. The actions and presentations catalogue also gives coefficient-sum relations over semirings and the precise universal properties, including their universe qualifications.

Fixed vectors and averaging

For monoid representations on additive commutative monoid modules over a semiring, intertwiners f, g with injective f and Function.Exact f g remain exact on fixed submodules. Fixed vectors commute with arbitrary products and algebraic direct sums, but do not in general preserve surjectivity.

Noetherian monoid cohomology. For A : Rep R G over any ring R and monoid G, inhomogeneous cochains have degree n term (Fin n → G) → A and differential using the given linear action. Degree zero is canonically R-linearly equivalent to A.ρ.fixedSubmodule. If IsNoetherian R A, degree-zero cohomology is Noetherian for arbitrary G; if also Finite G, cohomology is Noetherian in every natural degree. Neither commutativity nor Noetherianity of R, finiteness of the underlying A, nor a trivial action is required. The catalogue gives the independent universes and the narrower Mathlib group-cohomology comparison.

For a finite group whose order is a unit in a possibly noncommutative semiring, a given linear lift between intertwining maps can be replaced by an intertwining lift; no surjectivity of the map lifted through is required. Separately, for a finite group acting by additive automorphisms on an additive commutative group, bijective multiplication by the group order makes every crossed map principal. The fixed-vector and averaging catalogue keeps the representation and additive-action hypotheses distinct and gives the explicit averaging formula and its naturality.

A pretransitive group action on a finite nonempty set has permutation-fixed submodule linearly equivalent to the coefficient semiring; the acting group need not be finite and no invertible order is needed. A nonzero fixed vector forces the acted-on carrier to be finite; on an infinite pretransitive carrier the fixed submodule is bottom, still over any coefficient semiring. Separately, for a finite group whose order is a unit in a possibly noncommutative semiring, the normalized norm is a natural idempotent self-intertwiner with range the fixed submodule, on additive commutative monoid modules. Its narrower commutative-ring comparison with Mathlib's averageMap is in the catalogue.

Free groups, arithmetic and Grothendieck completions

For any generator relabeling σ : I → J, the kernel of FreeGroup.map σ is the normal closure of all of i * (of k)⁻¹ with σ i = σ k. The label types may be empty and lie in independent universes; no injectivity, surjectivity or finiteness is needed. The induced homomorphism is injective or surjective exactly when σ is. See the free-group map API.

For a finite-index subgroup H of a finitely generated free group G, rank H + [G:H] = rank G * [G:H] + 1, including ambient rank zero. Mathlib supplies subgroup freeness and finite generation. Biadditive maps from two additive commutative monoids to an additive commutative group extend uniquely to both Grothendieck completions; a semiring's additive completion has a canonical ring structure and universal ring lifts, without cancellation.

For a finitely generated additive commutative group M, a subgroup has finite index exactly when its canonical integer-module finrank equals Module.finrank ℤ M. The finite-index criterion requires [Module.Finite ℤ M], not freeness or torsion-freeness, and includes rank zero. The span of a linearly independent family of Module.finrank ℤ M elements has finite quotient and positive subgroup index.

For an additive commutative group V, subgroup L and natural n, bijective multiplication by n on V gives (V/L)[n] ≃+ L/nL. If L is finitely generated, this torsion subgroup is finite of cardinality n ^ Module.finrank ℤ L, without freeness of L or ambient finite generation. The free-group, arithmetic and completion catalogue also links the explicit index-two generators and nilpotence reflection on additive units.

Topology and abstract homomorphisms

For a subsemigroup S of a group with continuous fixed left translations, its topological closure is stable under inverses if that closure's induced topology is Noetherian and each right translation by a member of S is continuous on the closure. No ambient Noetherianity or continuous inversion is assumed. See Subsemigroup.inv_mem_closure_of_noetherianSpace and the precise inputs. Alternatively, with the same local right continuity, inverse stability follows if S is preirreducible and strict inclusion of nonempty irreducible closed subsets of the ambient group is well-founded (WellFoundedLT). This applies to every element of the closure, not just members of S.

More generally, a closed self-embedding of any topological space is surjective if the space has finitely many irreducible components and well-founded strict inclusion of nonempty irreducible closed subsets. No Noetherianity or separation is assumed; see the closed-embedding theorem.

In a compact Hausdorff commutative topological group with a dense algebraically finitely generated subgroup, every abstract finite-index subgroup is open. Every abstract homomorphism from such a source to a compact nonarchimedean group is continuous, even with an infinite image; this does not assert local constancy. The topology catalogue also covers ultrafilter-limit counterexamples to continuity, cofinite groups and actions, locally constructible subgroups, identity components, compact open subgroups and finite-coordinate factorizations.

Two continuity interfaces are separate: a pretransitive group action by continuous fixed-element maps on a finite T0 space makes its given topology discrete, with no topology or finiteness needed on the group. By contrast, for topological spaces M, X where every point of X has a finite neighborhood, a primitive scalar operation M × X → X is jointly continuous if both fixed-scalar maps and orbit maps are continuous; no action laws or separation are required. The catalogue includes the local finite-neighborhood criterion for arbitrary maps.

Pontryagin characters and bidual evaluation

  • Saturation of uniformly spanning character sets. Let K be a compact monoid with measurable opens and measurable fixed translations, and let μ be a finite nonzero left-invariant measure. If the complex span of the scalar characters from a set S is uniformly dense in C(K, ℂ), then S is the entire Pontryagin dual. In particular, every point-separating subgroup of characters is the whole dual. The measure need not be a probability measure; no group, Hausdorffness or countability assumption on K is required. See PontryaginDual.Saturation.
  • Uniform density of scalar characters. Let K be a compact monoid with a topology, and let S be a subgroup of its continuous Circle-valued characters that separates points of K. The complex linear span of x ↦ (χ x : ℂ), for χ ∈ S, is dense in C(K, ℂ) for the uniform topology. No measure, continuity of the monoid operations, or separation or countability typeclass on K is required. This uses Mathlib's scalar Stone–Weierstrass theorem; see GroupTheory.Topology.PontryaginDual.StoneWeierstrass.
  • Real characters and canonical biduality. Every continuous circle-valued character of the additive real line has a unique period-one Fourier frequency. PontryaginDual.realEquiv identifies the original real topology with the compact-open topology on the entire real character space. The forward map of PontryaginDual.realEvalEquiv is canonical bidual evaluation, not a separately chosen algebraic identification. See PontryaginDual.Real.
  • Transport of the whole Pontryagin dual. A continuous multiplicative equivalence A ≃ₜ* B induces PontryaginDual B ≃ₜ* PontryaginDual A by precomposition, with the original compact-open topologies. Only monoids with topologies are needed: no local compactness, Hausdorffness or continuity of their operations is assumed. See PontryaginDual.mapEquiv in PontryaginDual.
  • Canonical Pontryagin bidual evaluation. For a monoid A equipped with a topology and [LocallyCompactPair A Circle], PontryaginDual.eval A is the continuous homomorphism a ↦ (χ ↦ χ a) into the existing compact-open bidual. It is natural under arbitrary continuous monoid homomorphisms. If the dual also forms a locally compact pair with Circle, evaluation on the dual is a section of the dual of evaluation. No group, commutativity, continuity of the monoid operations or Hausdorff hypothesis on A is required. These general monoid results alone do not assert injectivity, surjectivity or full Pontryagin reflexivity of the original evaluation. See GroupTheory.Topology.PontryaginDual.
  • Bidual evaluation separates discrete abelian groups. For a commutative group with its discrete topology, PontryaginDual.eval_injective_of_discrete proves that the canonical continuous bidual evaluation is injective. No finiteness, finite generation or countability is required. The injectivity theorem is available in PontryaginDual.Discrete. For full canonical continuous reflexivity, including the inverse, use PontryaginDual.evalEquivOfDiscrete in PontryaginDual.Discrete.Equivalence.
  • Integrals and orthogonality of characters. For a monoid K with its original topology and measurable space, measurable opens and [MeasurableMul K], a nontrivial continuous circle-valued character has complex Bochner integral zero against any left-invariant measure. Distinct characters have zero off-diagonal integral ∫ x, χ x * conj (ψ x) ∂μ. No group, compactness, finiteness or normalization of the measure is assumed; for a nonintegrable function the Bochner integral is zero by definition. See PontryaginDual.integral_eq_zero_of_ne_one and integral_mul_conj_eq_zero in PontryaginDual.Integral.
