• Uniqueness of finite biproduct factors. In a preadditive category, two finite families with local canonical, possibly noncommutative endomorphism rings and isomorphic biproducts have equivalent index types and matching isomorphic factors. Only the two given biproducts are needed, with independent object, morphism and both index universes; no abelian structure, chain conditions or idempotent completeness is assumed. Repeated factors are allowed; the matching need not be unique or make the supplied isomorphism diagonal. Statement and module.
  • Finite indecomposable decompositions. An Artinian object, or separately a Noetherian object, in a category with zero morphisms and finite biproducts is isomorphic to a finite biproduct of nonzero indecomposables; zero has the empty decomposition. No preadditivity, abelian structure, second chain condition or idempotent completeness is needed; this is existence, not uniqueness. Statements and modules.
  • Finite-length decompositions with local endomorphism rings. A Noetherian and Artinian object in any abelian category is isomorphic to a finite biproduct with local canonical, possibly noncommutative endomorphism rings on its factors. Object and morphism universes are independent; zero has the empty decomposition. This asserts existence and locality, not uniqueness. Its local-End factors also satisfy the generic uniqueness theorem above. Statement and module.
  • Cancellation of finite local-End families. In a preadditive category, a finite biproduct of objects with local canonical, possibly noncommutative endomorphism rings cancels from binary biproducts with arbitrary remaining objects. Only the common full sum and the two binary totals are required; no intermediate sums, chain conditions or idempotent completeness are assumed. The object, morphism and index universes are independent, and the empty family is allowed. The residual isomorphism is not canonical. Statement and module.
  • Cancellation of finite-length summands. In any abelian category, if X is Noetherian and Artinian, X ⊞ Y ≅ X ⊞ Z implies Nonempty (Y ≅ Z) for unrestricted Y, Z. No locality of End X is required; zero is allowed and the isomorphism is not canonical. Statement and module.
  • Subobject correspondence and modularity. Canonical subobject lattices in any abelian category are modular. Pullback along an epimorphism identifies codomain subobjects with domain subobjects containing its kernel, with direct image as inverse; the cokernel case is included. No smallness is assumed. Statements and module.
  • Composition series and Jordan–Hölder. A Noetherian and Artinian object in any abelian category has a finite composition series from ⊥ to ⊤ with simple quotient factors. Any two given series with equal heads and lasts have equal lengths and isomorphic factors after a bijection, without chain conditions or bottom/top assumptions for uniqueness. Statements and module.
  • Categorical object length. In any category, objectLength X : ℕ∞ measures strict steps in the canonical subobject order and is invariant under isomorphisms and monotone under monomorphisms. In an abelian category it is additive on every short exact sequence, including infinite lengths, and is finite exactly for Noetherian and Artinian objects. Full composition series compute it; over any ring it agrees with Module.length, with independent scalar and carrier universes. Statements and modules.
  • Fitting decomposition and intertwiners. For an endomorphism of a Noetherian and Artinian object in any abelian category, all sufficiently high powers have complementary kernels and images, with canonical copairing an isomorphism. The original endomorphism has a nilpotent restriction to the kernel and an invertible restriction to the image, with a common-tail block-intertwining equation. Arbitrary intertwiners between two such objects are block diagonal in these canonical coordinates on one common tail. Complementarity agrees with linear-map power kernels and ranges over arbitrary, possibly noncommutative rings. Separately, generic block-intertwiner and mixed-Hom vanishing laws need only zero morphisms and the displayed biproducts, nilpotence and mono/epi conditions, not abelian structure. Statements and modules.
  • Canonical finite-length power bounds. If objectLength X ≠ ⊤, put L = (objectLength X).toNat. Power kernels stabilize by L with zero morphisms and kernels; nilpotent endomorphisms satisfy g ^ L = 0. Power images stabilize by L with images and image maps. On a Noetherian and Artinian object in an abelian category, kernels and images of f ^ n agree with those of the Drazin projector for every n ≥ L; f ^ (L + 1) * drazinInverse f = f ^ L and (f * (1 - f * drazinInverse f)) ^ L = 0. Zero length is included; these are uniform mathematical bounds, not least exponents or runtime bounds. Over any ring, a module of finite composition length L has ker (f ^ n) = ker (f ^ L) for every endomorphism and every n ≥ L; finite generation alone is not the hypothesis. Equations and hypotheses.
  • Canonical Drazin inverses. Every endomorphism of a Noetherian and Artinian object in an abelian category has a unique commuting Drazin inverse. Its characteristic equations, nilpotent and idempotent cases, and preservation by arbitrary multiplicative maps and functors are included. For preservation both relevant objects must be Noetherian and Artinian; functors need not be additive or exact. Intertwiners also intertwine the inverses, and taking the inverse respects every natural power, including zero, and products of commuting endomorphisms. Double inversion gives the group part f * drazinInverse f * f, not generally f. Equations and hypotheses.
  • Drazin sums, projectors and perturbations. On a Noetherian and Artinian object in an abelian category, mutually annihilating endomorphisms in both product orders have additive Drazin inverses, also on finite supports. A commuting nilpotent perturbation preserves the stable-image projector and has an explicit finite geometric inverse formula; the Drazin inverse itself generally changes. The projector has the stable power kernel and image; f * (1 - f * drazinInverse f) is nilpotent, not necessarily the complementary idempotent itself. These are conditional laws, not general additivity or perturbation invariance of the inverse. Formulas and hypotheses.
  • Cyclic and triangular Drazin formulas. Between two Noetherian and Artinian objects of an abelian category, arbitrary a : X ⟶ Y, b : Y ⟶ X satisfy Cline's cyclic-product formula, with a canonical isomorphism between the images of their Drazin projectors. For such X, Y, a unit f : End X, nilpotent g : End Y with any witness g ^ n = 0, and arbitrary h : X ⟶ Y give a finite-sum formula for the inverse of Biprod.ofComponents f h 0 g; no intertwining condition on h is imposed. The canonical image of its stable-image projector is isomorphic to X, with the isomorphism's inverse given by a graph map. The witness may be zero or nonminimal. Separately, cyclic powers need only a category, and cyclic nilpotence only zero morphisms. Cyclic formulas, triangular formula.
  • Sylvester equation and invariant graphs. In any preadditive category, invertible f : X ⟶ X, nilpotent g : End Y and arbitrary h : X ⟶ Y give a unique u : X ⟶ Y with f ≫ u = h + u ≫ g. No biproducts, scalar action or chain conditions are needed; object and morphism universes are independent. Under the abelian, Noetherian and Artinian triangular hypotheses above, this identifies the canonical graph slope, including its factor of f, and the unique section of the first projection that intertwines f with the triangular endomorphism. Equation and canonical graph.
  • Nilpotence in short complexes. For a compatible endomorphism of a short exact complex in any category with zero morphisms, the middle component is nilpotent exactly when both outer components are. Exactness alone gives the explicit middle exponent m + n from outer exponents m, n; a monic first arrow and an epic second arrow reflect a middle exponent to the left and right components, respectively, without changing it. All natural exponents, including zero, are allowed; no abelian, additive, splitting or chain-condition premise is needed. Statements and module.
  • Local endomorphism rings. An indecomposable Noetherian and Artinian object in any abelian category has a local canonical, possibly noncommutative endomorphism ring; every endomorphism is nilpotent or a unit. Indecomposability excludes zero, with no extra nontriviality premise. Statements and module.
  • Indecomposability from locality. In any preadditive category with binary biproducts, a local canonical ring End X implies Mathlib's original Indecomposable X. No abelian structure, chain conditions, chosen zero object or separate nonzero hypothesis is required; the ring may be noncommutative. Statement and module.
  • Finite Euler identities. In an abelian category, an invariant valued in any additive commutative group and additive on every native short exact sequence gives finite integer-interval Euler identities for chains and cochains with explicit endpoint-image corrections. Quasi-isomorphism invariance requires both complexes to have term support in the same nonempty interval; homology-sum SES additivity for chains requires this of all three complexes. Cochain LES additivity instead has connecting-image corrections removable by neighboring homology vanishing. Exact-functor transport, finite-support and native-shift formulas, and length for finitely generated modules over a commutative Noetherian Artinian ring are included. Formulas and hypotheses, Euler guide.
  • Unrestricted Hom complexes and K-objects. For integer cochains in any preadditive category, the canonical Hom complex uses unrestricted products of abelian groups, not products in the coefficient category. Homotopies act in both variables; a contraction in either contracts the Hom complex, and degree-n cohomology vanishes exactly when every map K ⟶ L⟦n⟧ is null-homotopic. In an abelian category, K-projectivity and K-injectivity are characterized by acyclicity of Hom complexes against acyclic tests, even by vanishing in one fixed degree. They descend along one-sided homotopy retracts, are invariant under categorical equivalences, and are detected componentwise on supplied coproducts and products, respectively, including empty families. Hom-complex quasi-isomorphism criteria, diagonal homotopy criteria with evaluation-preservation hypotheses, and canonical preimages of supplied homotopy equivalences under fully faithful additive functors complement these results; no boundedness or enough-projectives/injectives assumption is imposed. Detailed statements and modules, Hom-complex guide.
  • Ordinary disks and bounded-below projective chains. In a category with zero morphisms and a zero object, an integer cochain identity disk in degrees n,n+1 is ordinarily projective or injective exactly when its coefficient is; no preadditivity is required. Separately, in any abelian category, a particular map between integer chain complexes with projective terms and possibly different termwise lower bounds is a quasi-isomorphism exactly when it is the forward map of a homotopy equivalence. There is no upper-bound or enough-projectives assumption. Ordinary projectivity is distinct from K-projectivity. Statements and modules.
  • Chain conditions and homology. A functor between arbitrary categories preserving monomorphisms and reflecting isomorphisms reflects Noetherian and, separately, Artinian objects, with four independent universes; fullness and faithfulness are not required. In an abelian category, a short complex with Noetherian middle object has Noetherian homology. Epimorphic Noetherian and monomorphic Artinian endomorphisms are isomorphisms. Both module criteria use the original scalar action over any ring, with independent scalar and carrier universes. No smallness, well-poweredness, Noetherian-ring or whole-category chain condition is assumed. In an abelian category, binary biproducts preserve each chain condition separately from that condition on both factors. Statements and modules.
  • Quotients, admissible layers and snake sequences. In any abelian category, for subobjects L ≤ U of B and any D, the chosen-cokernel map U/(L ⊓ D) ⟶ (U/L) × (B/D) is monic, without L ≤ D. Layers admissible for a quotient property P have reversed lower and forward upper order; subobject and binary-product closure and P(B/D) make the lower cut (L,U) ↦ (L ⊓ D,U) left adjoint to inclusion of layers with lower endpoint at most D. Separately, snake inputs with exact rows, epic upper second arrow and monic lower first arrow give an eight-object sequence exact at all six interior objects. Neither exterior object is required to vanish; the four middle exactness positions use Mathlib's snake lemma. Statements and modules, quotient guide, layer guide.
  • Fixed-arrow relative paths. For integer cochains in a preadditive category with binary biproducts and pullbacks, the pullback of a fixed f : B₁ ⟶ B along the first native path endpoint has a strict section, lifts from chosen homotopies with strict endpoint equations, and a homotopy equivalence to B₁ whose inverse homotopy vanishes over the first projection. The object depends only on f; no arbitrary-arrow naturality or new quasi-isomorphism instance is asserted. Statement and module, relative-path guide.
  • Quadrant product totals. For any ring R, native quadrant bicomplexes in ModuleCat.{u} R, with R : Type u, have signed unrestricted product totals in integer degrees p-r. Acyclic horizontal rows at every row and horizontal degree, including the boundary p=0, imply total acyclicity in all integer degrees. Chosen elementwise lifts are not linear or natural splittings; this is not an arbitrary-unbounded total theorem. Statement and module, product-total guide.
