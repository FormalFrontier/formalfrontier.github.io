The families below summarize the library. The complete catalogue retains the detailed statements, qualifiers and source links; the usage guide provides the module and declaration inventories.

  • Single terms and augmentation. Over a nontrivial semiring and any monoid, IsUnit (single m r) ↔ IsUnit r ∧ IsUnit m; coefficient-unit reflection also allows the zero semiring. Over any semiring and an index type with only multiplication, a single term is left-regular iff its coefficient is left-regular and either the coefficients are subsingleton or its index is left-regular; the right-hand criterion is analogous. Augmentation is the coefficient-sum ring homomorphism over any semiring. Over a ring its kernel is the left span of single m 1 - 1, with monoid or subgroup generating-set refinements. MonoidAlgebra.augmentationKerBasis is a basis over the coefficient ring R, not over R[M], indexed by nonidentity monoid elements. Statements and modules.
  • Coefficient tests, centers and finite matrices. Over any possibly zero or noncommutative semiring, a group-algebra element is determined by one chosen coefficient after all right multiplications, and independently after all left multiplications. Centrality means central coefficients invariant under conjugation; coefficients vanish on infinite conjugacy orbits. For any monoid, finite decidable matrix indices give the ring equivalence MonoidAlgebra (Matrix n n R) M ≃+* Matrix n n (R[M]), preserving both multiplication orders, including empty indices. Precise statements.
  • Crossed maps and averaging. Over any ring and monoid, linear maps from the full augmentation ideal to a given left R[M]-module correspond additively to normalized crossed maps z(mn) = z(m) + single m 1 • z(n) for its canonical action. For a finite group of unit order in the coefficient ring, every crossed map is principal, with negative inverse-order sum witness, natural for additive maps. See the universal property and averaging.
  • Augmentation cotangents. For any unital ring and monoid, coefficient-linear maps from I/I² to a left R-module V correspond as a set to M →* Multiplicative V. For a group, I/I² ≃ₗ[R] R ⊗[ℤ] Additive (Abelianization G), where I is the augmentation ideal and I² its ordinary ideal square. Coefficient covariance is semilinear; monoid products split by projections and sums of inclusions. No commutativity or freeness is required. Statements · cotangent guide.
  • Finite p-groups and fixed vectors. For prime p, any ring with (p : R) = 0 and finite p-group G, the augmentation ideal has zero Nat.card G-th power. Every nontrivial unital R[G]-module has a nonzero canonical fixed vector; simplicity is equivalent to simplicity under canonical coefficient restriction, and simple modules have trivial group action. For any group over any ring, augmentation nilpotence forces Subsingleton R ∨ Finite G, without a characteristic assumption. If (p : R) = 0, nilpotence is equivalent to Subsingleton R ∨ (Finite G ∧ IsPGroup p G); over nontrivial R it also forces one common p-power exponent. For locally finite p-groups over any ring with (p : R) = 0, every augmentation-zero element is nilpotent, with an element-dependent exponent; no uniform kernel power is asserted. Under these same locally finite hypotheses, the Jacobson radical is the augmentation preimage of the coefficient radical. In particular, a single term is radical exactly when its coefficient is, and a zero coefficient radical gives the augmentation kernel. See the radical guide and statements and guides.
  • Given modules and scalar restriction. Over any semiring and monoid, canonical representations and an additive Hom/intertwiner correspondence retain the given carriers and coefficient actions. For finite groups of unit order in the coefficient semiring, coefficient projectivity implies group-algebra projectivity. The normalized norm is natural for R[G]-linear maps and is the unique R[G]-linear retraction onto the fixed vectors' span. For rings S, A, a map φ : S →+* A surjective modulo a two-sided ideal I, and an A-module V with ∃ n, I^n ≤ Module.annihilator A V, the exact restriction Module.compHom V φ preserves and reflects both chain conditions and preserves length in ℕ∞, including infinity. Semisimplicity is equivalent to annihilation by I and semisimplicity of that restriction. For finite p-groups over rings with prime p = 0, this gives the canonical coefficient comparisons: semisimplicity requires coefficient semisimplicity and trivial group action. Fixed vectors detect injectivity of module maps; coinvariants detect surjectivity, without finite generation. Statements and modules.
  • Finiteness of modules. For rings S, A, compatible scalar towers, and a two-sided ideal I, a Noetherian A-module with uniformly nilpotent I-action is finite over S whenever A ⧸ I is finite over S; no surjective scalar map or nilpotence of I itself is needed. Separately, a simple algebra over a commutative ring admitting a nontrivial compatible module Noetherian over that base is itself Noetherian, hence finite, over the base; the base ring need not be Noetherian. A Noetherian A-semimodule also has a greatest A-submodule finite over compatible scalars S, preserved by every A-linear endomorphism. For the regular module this is a two-sided ideal. Both scalar semirings may be noncommutative, and S need not be Noetherian. Greatest finite submodules · other statements and hypotheses.
  • Subgroup ideals and coset bases. Over any ring, a subgroup's left difference ideal consists of elements whose coefficient sums vanish on left cosets gH. Over nontrivial rings these ideals reflect subgroup membership, inclusion, finite generation and normality via two-sidedness. Separately, over any semiring, R[G] has a left R[H]-basis indexed by right cosets H * g; finite index gives module finiteness. No normality is required for these coset constructions. Statements · subgroup guide.
  • Coefficient and index quotients. Over semirings, extension of any left coefficient ideal is characterized coefficientwise. For any semiring homomorphism and monoid, coefficient change preserves and reflects zero kernel powers at each fixed exponent, including zero, without surjectivity. For Ring R, Semiring S, Group G, Monoid M, and maps f : R →+* S, φ : G →* M, the mixed kernel is the sum of the extended coefficient kernel and the kernel-subgroup difference ideal. For arbitrary maps the quotient is the composite's range subsemiring; surjective f gives S[MonoidHom.mrange φ], and surjectivity of both gives S[M]. Normal-subgroup quotients and unchanged coefficient kernel/range quotients have full basis and factorization laws. Statements and guides.
  • Commuting scalar actions and central idempotents. Commuting ring actions on a module remain commuting after quotienting both rings by annihilating ideals; two-sidedness is needed for quotient rings, not just their scalar actions. For any ring A and two-sided ideal I, the first ideal-square quotient I/I² has commuting left and right A ⧸ I-actions. In any ring, an idempotent central modulo a nilpotent two-sided ideal I is central if both off-diagonal corner maps send I into I²; quotient centrality alone is insufficient. Actions · centrality criterion.
  • Central summands detected by characters. For a commutative ring R and an R-algebra A Artinian on both sides, every χ : A →ₐ[R] R is one on a central idempotent whose principal left ideal is finite over R with its inherited action. Zero and nonreduced base rings are allowed; the whole algebra need not be finite over R. Statement and proof strategy.
  • Finite presentation. For any commutative ring R and finitely generated commutative monoid M, the canonical R-algebra R[M] is finitely presented. The additive-monoid version is also an instance. Coefficients need not be Noetherian, and exponents need not be cancellative or finite. Module · statements and use.
  • Chain conditions and regularity. For any ring R and finitely generated commutative monoid M, R[M] is left-Noetherian iff R is. For a commutative group G, the exact criterion is left-Noetherianity of R and Subsingleton R ∨ Group.FG G. A finite-index finitely generated commutative subgroup gives the coefficient-ring criterion even when the ambient group is noncommutative and the subgroup is not normal. For a cancellative monoid over any semiring, a nonzero left ideal finite over the coefficients forces the monoid to be finite. Additive right-compatible coefficient retractions give left-Artinian descent without multiplicativity. Such a retraction exists for every homomorphism from a division ring to a nontrivial semiring, without centrality or finite-dimensionality assumptions. Left-Noetherianity descends to embedded group algebras over any semiring; finite-index subgroup algebras over rings share it with the whole algebra. Over nontrivial rings it forces every subgroup to be finitely generated, without a general converse. The infinite cyclic and finite-index cyclic criteria reduce to the coefficient ring; import GroupRings.MonoidAlgebra.CyclicNoetherian directly for these. Over any ring, infinite-order elements of cancellative monoids give regular monic basis differences, forcing torsion in left-Artinian monoid algebras with nontrivial coefficients. For any ring R and either a left-cancellative or a right-cancellative monoid G, MonoidAlgebra.isArtinianRing_iff_of_isLeftCancelMul and MonoidAlgebra.isArtinianRing_iff give that R[G] is left-Artinian iff R is left-Artinian and Subsingleton R ∨ Finite G. The corresponding finite_of_isArtinianRing theorems give monoid finiteness directly over any nontrivial ring, specializing to the classical group criterion. Locally, over a nontrivial semiring and any monoid, left Artinianity makes every right-regular index a unit; over a nontrivial ring, it also makes every left-regular index a unit, without global cancellation. The global criteria require ring coefficients. All these conditions use the original left regular action. The zero ring is allowed in the criterion, the universes are independent, and no commutativity or right-Artinian hypothesis is imposed. Separately, LaurentPolynomial R is left-Artinian iff R is subsingleton, even for noncommutative or zero rings. Statements and modules.
  • Laurent coordinates. Supplied coordinates G ≃+ (σ →₀ ℤ) identify an additive commutative group's algebra over a commutative semiring with MvLaurentPolynomial σ A. Free abelian groups have canonical coordinates, with arbitrary, empty or infinite variable types. Statements and module.
  • Characters and right convolution. Character evaluations on an additive commutative group G are linearly independent over any field K, even for infinite groups without enough roots. For finite G, enough roots of unity of exponent Monoid.exponent (Multiplicative G) give a character basis; with [Fintype G] they give diagonalization and rank for right convolution, whose entry is f (-sigma + tau) and eigenvalue is ∑ x, f x * psi x with positive sign. The eigenvector law needs no enough-roots assumption. Statements · Fourier guide.
