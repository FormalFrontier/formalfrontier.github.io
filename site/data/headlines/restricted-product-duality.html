• Evaluation with a compact open subgroup. For a Hausdorff abelian topological group with a compact open subgroup, canonical Circle-valued compact-open evaluation is surjective and open on the entire bidual. Evaluation identifies the compact subgroup homeomorphically with the open annihilator of the restriction kernel. No countability or metrizability hypothesis is required. Evaluation
  • Compact containment and finite factorization. Every compact subset of a T1 restricted product lies in one finite-exceptional stage, for arbitrary distinguished subsets and index type; only the original restricted product, not the factors, must be T1. The open-distinguished-subset theorem instead needs openness, not T1. Continuous maps from compact spaces to a T1 singleton cofinite product factor by ordinary finite coordinate restriction. No compact stages or countable exhaustion are asserted. Containment, factorization.
  • Ordinary singleton stages and coordinatewise path components. Restriction identifies a singleton principal stage on any free set with its ordinary Pi product; the inverse inserts the chosen points elsewhere. Finite insertions embed and detect continuity out of the singleton cofinite product. Its stock path-component quotient is canonically equivalent as a set to the cofinite product of the factor path-component quotients, with finite inverse reconstruction; no separation or connected-component assertion. Stages, path components.
  • Factorwise connectivity and quotient maps. For a given family b i, PointProduct b is path connected iff every factor is path connected, and locally path connected iff every factor is locally path connected (no path connectedness is needed for the latter); forward instances are provided. Raw coordinate evaluation and the whole insertion map from the topological sum Σ s : Finset ι, ∀ j : s, X j are quotient maps. Individual finite insertions are embeddings, not generally surjective. Connectivity.
  • Finite-stage colimit. For given points b i, the original singleton cofinite restricted product is the colimit in TopCat of its ordinary finite Pi products under insertion of the given points. Compatible continuous stage maps descend uniquely, with computation on every finite insertion. The direct continuous-map API allows an independent, unbased observer universe; categorical cocones belong to the fixed TopCat universe of the diagram. No separation or nonempty-index assumption is needed. Colimit and descent.
  • Positive homotopy groups have finite support. For a T1 original singleton cofinite restricted product, coordinate evaluation gives a multiplicative equivalence with the bottom-subgroup restricted product of the factor homotopy groups, hence with Multiplicative (DFinsupp (fun i ↦ Additive (HomotopyGroup N (R i) (b i)))). The cube index N is arbitrary nonempty with decidable equality, not necessarily finite; no factor T1, CW/CGWH or classification assumption. Comparison, DFinsupp composite.
  • Based maps and empty-dimensional naturality. The based-postcomposition API is provided by Fundamental Groupoids and re-exported here. For arbitrary spaces, chosen points x, y and continuous f with f x = y, HomotopyGroup.mapFun N f hf acts on the generalized-loop quotient for any cube-index type N, with independent source, target and cube universes. Representative, constant-class, identity, composition and relative-to-{x} homotopy laws need no decidable equality or nonemptiness of N. Under the given positive-index dictionaries it is the existing homomorphism's function. This library supplies the comparison with ZerothHomotopy.map, also provided by Fundamental Groupoids: for empty N, including Fin 0, the canonical path-component comparison intertwines the two maps. Discrete Bool negation computes two distinct quotient/path classes, four comparison values and the off-basepoint square, not a nontrivial positive homotopy group. Maps on homotopy quotients and zeroth naturality.
  • Based naturality and joint homotopies. Continuous coordinate maps preserving every chosen point give both comparison squares. The path-component square needs no separation; the positive square requires T1 of both original products. Based postcomposition, its homomorphisms and coordinate HomotopyRels assembling jointly need no T1; based-homotopic families induce equal maps. Naturality, joint homotopies.
  • Based coordinate equivalences and contractions. Homeomorphisms carrying each b i to c i give product homeomorphisms. Based continuous f i, g i with both inverse homotopies relative to {b i}, {c i} give product homotopy equivalences. Chosen factor homotopies id → const (b i) fixing b i give a contraction and ContractibleSpace; mere unbased factor contractibility is not the input. None requires separation or finite index. Homeomorphisms, homotopy equivalences, contractions.
  • Algebraic finite-support coordinates. At bottom submonoids, MulOneClass factors give a multiplicative equivalence with Multiplicative (DFinsupp (fun i ↦ Additive (G i))). Bundled evaluation and family maps use Monoid; bottom-subgroup products use Group. No topology or commutativity is needed; this is not a noncommutative categorical coproduct. Finite support.
  • Compact abelian character separation. For a compact Hausdorff abelian topological group in any universe, every nonidentity element is detected by a continuous Circle-valued character. No additional countability, probability normalization or measurable-space input is required. Character separation.
  • Compact Pontryagin biduality. For a compact Hausdorff commutative topological group in any universe, the original evaluation map is a continuous multiplicative equivalence with its entire compact-open Pontryagin bidual. Its inverse reconstructs every character value of a bidual element. Evaluation and reconstruction commute with every continuous multiplicative morphism between such groups, with independent source and target universes. This does not assert biduality for arbitrary locally compact abelian groups. Compact biduality.
  • Conditional character reconstruction and duality. For commutative groups, local circle bicharacters and orthogonality on both distinguished subgroups give a finite-product pairing. Joint local continuity and both subgroup families open give a jointly continuous pairing and continuous dual map; local separation gives injectivity, not surjectivity. A global continuous character kills a finite-coordinate tail of the distinguished product. Reconstruction requires bijective actual right local maps onto all algebraic circle characters and exact right annihilator = V, not merely continuous-character perfectness or nondegeneracy. Topological equivalence additionally needs finite discrete character-domain X i and topological representing Y i. Transposition requires finite discrete Y i, topological X i, left local perfectness and exact left annihilator; this is not general biduality or open mapping. Pairing, continuity, reconstruction, transpose.
  • Contravariant duality naturality. Adjoint continuous coordinate homomorphisms give the pairing/dual-map square with subgroup preservation only cofinitely (exceptional failures allowed). The equivalence square retains its stronger local-perfectness, exact-annihilator and finite-discrete assumptions. Naturality.
