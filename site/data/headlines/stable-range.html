These results use Mathlib. The quasiregular-ideal predicate and unit characterization come from pinned general-linear-groups; the stable-range consequences are proved here. The guide retains detailed statements, coordinate formulas, compatibility names and client inventories.

  • Bass conditions. For arbitrary possibly noncommutative rings: right-unimodular rows, (S_n), least stable index, monotonicity, ring-equivalence invariance and surjective descent/quasiregular quotients. Nontrivial local rings have least stable range one.
  • Local interfaces. Division semirings are local with zero nonunits submonoid; the maximal-ideal additive comparison requires a commutative local semiring. For rings, locality is equivalent to Jacobson-radical maximality among all left ideals. In local rings the radical is the nonunits and contains every proper left ideal; these statements do not assume two-sidedness of the left ideals.
  • Preservation. Products preserve every (S_n). Semisimple rings and semisimple radical quotients satisfy (S₁) without radical nilpotence. Finite matrix rings, idempotent corners and opposites preserve (S₁); matrix reflection needs nonempty indices, corners neither centrality nor fullness. Commutative semilocal rings satisfy (S₁) assuming only finite maximal spectrum, including the zero ring.
  • Retract endomorphisms. In any preadditive category, i : X ⟶ Z, p : Z ⟶ X and i ≫ p = 𝟙 X transfer Bass.StableRangeCondition (End Z) 1 to Bass.StableRangeCondition (End X) 1 for the original canonical endomorphism rings. Object and morphism universes are independent; no complementary object, finite-length or nonzero hypothesis is needed.
  • Finite biproduct endomorphisms. In any preadditive category, a displayed finite biproduct of objects with local endomorphism rings, and every displayed retract of that biproduct, have canonical endomorphism rings satisfying (S₁). Only that biproduct and, for the retract, its supplied retraction are required, with independent index universe; empty families and the zero biproduct are included. No vanishing of morphisms between summands is assumed.
  • Regularity and cancellation. (S₁) implies direct finiteness, is equivalent to unit-valued pair shortening, and under von Neumann regularity is equivalent to unit-regularity. Biproduct cancellation in any preadditive category needs (S₁) for the canonical End(X) and only the two displayed biproducts. The remaining objects are unrestricted; local End is sufficient, and neither global sums nor commutativity is assumed. Module cancellation is a specialization, with independent carrier universes and no finiteness or projectivity; regular right modules use the opposite ring. Division-ring vector-space endomorphisms have inner inverses; finite dimension gives automorphic inner inverses and unit-regular finite matrix rings, including empty indices.
  • Ranks and blocks. Division-ring row rank has rectangular bounds, reindexing, realization and repeated-block laws; normalized empty square rank is zero. Repeated-block homomorphisms work over NonAssocSemiring coefficients, injectively for nonempty block families.
  • Commutative dimension bounds. Regularity is equivalent to reducedness and dimension at most zero; all modules over such regular rings are flat. Dimension at most zero gives (S₁) without reducedness or nontriviality; commutative regular rings are unit-regular. Noetherian dimension at most d gives (S_(d+1)), with no infinite-residue-fields premise.
  • Specified splittings. A supplied linear right inverse of a linear map f gives ker f × N ≃ₗ[R] M over a semiring, for an additive commutative group M and additive commutative monoid N, in independent universes. The prescribed complementary endomorphism id - g.comp f has range exactly ker f. Complementary-range ranks over a division ring satisfy finrank K (range (id - g.comp f)) = finrank K M - finrank K N assuming only that M is finite-dimensional. A family with given equivalences N b ≃ₗ[K] F has this rank equal to finrank K M - finrank K F at every b, without a nonempty base or a finite-dimensionality assumption on F. Right-unimodular coefficient rows split over Rᵐᵒᵖ for any ring, including empty rows and the zero ring. Splitting alone does not give freeness.
  • Completion and IBN. Right-unimodularity and free right kernels characterize two-sided rectangular completion, preserving a supplied inverse column. For successor-length rows, square completion needs IBN only to infer kernel size; supplied matching coordinates and the converse need no IBN. The countable endomorphism example separates rectangular from square completion for nontrivial underlying modules, even for a unary row.
  • Commutative cancellation. Under (S_s), right-unimodular rows of length at least s + 1 have free kernels. Finite-free cancellation uses an explicit P × R^m ≃ R^(n+m) and s ≤ n, without prior freeness of P; completion preserves the literal first row. Both allow zero rings; dimension-bound variants require Noetherianity. Two-entry completion retains supplied Bézout coefficients over commutative rings, with explicit scalar kernel coordinates.
  • Elementary matrices and determinants. For commutative rings satisfying (S₁), elementary diagonalization works in all finite ranks, including zero. Determinant generation identifies invertible determinant-one matrices with elementary matrices, the stable elementary subgroup with the determinant kernel, and its quotient with units, without locality, nontriviality or positive-rank hypotheses.
