• Upper-triangular semiring nilpotence. For an arbitrary, possibly noncommutative Semiring R and an independently-universed finite linearly ordered index type, an upper triangular matrix is nilpotent if and only if every diagonal entry is nilpotent. Given e : ι → ℕ killing the corresponding diagonal entries, the bound is A ^ (∑ i, e i) = 0, including empty indices and zero exponents. The strict Fin n predicate uses only Zero; its support bound holds over NonUnitalNonAssocSemiring and its nilpotence over NonUnitalSemiring. Positive-power index n means n + 1 factors, not an exact nth-power bound. The unitization and relative general linear constructions require NonUnitalRing. Upper-triangular nilpotence, nonunital powers and strict triangularity.
  • Matrix ideal-power bounds and nilpotence. For a Semiring R, a left ideal I : Ideal R and a square matrix A on an independently-universed finite decidable index type, entrywise membership in I gives (A ^ k) i j ∈ I ^ k. Any exponent annihilating the whole ideal also annihilates the matrix, so IsNilpotent I implies IsNilpotent A. Empty indices and zero semirings are included, without two-sidedness or commutativity. Powers follow the ordered recursions A ^ (k + 1) = A ^ k * A and I ^ (k + 1) = I ^ k * I; pointwise nilness of an arbitrary ideal does not give a uniform exponent. For a CommSemiring R, however, a finite square matrix with nilpotent entries is nilpotent: its finitely generated entry ideal is contained in the nilradical and is therefore nilpotent. This needs no Noetherian hypothesis and includes empty indices and zero semirings. Power bound and nilpotence, nilpotent entries, nonzero dual-number diagonal, nonzero matrix over ZMod 4.
  • Kronecker products of matrix units. Over any Semiring, the Kronecker product of two unit matrices is a unit, for independently-universed finite decidable square indices, including empty indices and zero semirings. The ordered factorization (A ⊗ₖ 1) * (1 ⊗ₖ B), for chosen input units with stored inverse values A' and B', supplies the reversed inverse witness (1 ⊗ₖ B') * (A' ⊗ₖ 1); tensoring input inverses is not valid in general over noncommutative coefficients. The rectangular mixed identity needs only a NonAssocSemiring and finite, decidable middle indices. Over commutative coefficients, the extracted unit agrees with mathlib's general linear Kronecker construction. Unit preservation, operational examples.
  • Single-entry matrix corners. For a Semiring R, finite decidable indices and a chosen index i, the corner of Matrix ι ι R at the diagonal matrix unit single i i 1 is ring-equivalent to R by extracting the (i,i) entry; its inverse inserts a coefficient there. The corner identity is the matrix unit, which need not be the ambient matrix identity. The equivalence also covers noncommutative and zero semirings. Corner equivalence, concrete clients.
  • Whitehead factorization and doubled lifting. Over any Ring, the block diagonal diag(g, g⁻¹) of a unit is elementary. A surjective ring homomorphism lifts this doubled block as a unit; it need not lift the original same-rank unit. For separately finite block indices, explicitly supplied diagonal units also give rectangular upper-triangular units with ordered inverse block -(A⁻¹ * B * D⁻¹). Factorization, doubled lift, elementary membership, rectangular guide.
  • Stable general linear and elementary groups. Initial-segment stabilization defines a coherent system of finite general linear groups over any Semiring, whose noncommutative direct limit has finite representatives, an eventual equality criterion, compatible lifts and coefficient maps. Over any Ring, the directed union of finite elementary images is the stable commutator subgroup; it is perfect in its own right and normal in stable general linear groups. Its quotient is canonically the abelianization, naturally under coefficient maps. No finite ambient elementary normality or commutativity hypothesis is needed. Stable general linear group, stable elementary group, boundary and quotient clients.
  • Stable determinant. Over a commutative ring, finite determinants induce homomorphisms on stable general linear groups, their elementary quotient and their abelianization. Rank-one units provide determinant sections; the elementary subgroup lies in the determinant kernel. The abelian elementary quotient splits as the product of its determinant kernel and the units, naturally under coefficient maps; this does not assert a product splitting of stable general linear groups. For commutative local rings, elementary matrices equal the determinant kernel, and the existing quotient determinant is an equivalence with the units. The rank-one map itself is defined over any semiring. Determinant and rank-one maps, integer and boundary examples.
  • Zero-product elementary stabilization. For rectangular A, B over any Ring with B * A = 0, the unit 1 + A * B has inverse 1 - A * B. Stabilizing by the identity on the other block gives exactly the ordered commutator of the upper shear of A and lower shear of B, hence an elementary unit. Both index types may be empty. Construction and factorization, integer and boundary examples.
  • Rectangular complement isomorphisms. In any preadditive category C : Type u with Category.{v} C, for arbitrary objects X, Y and morphisms a : X ⟶ Y, b : Y ⟶ X, the complements are simultaneously isomorphisms: IsIso (𝟙 X - a ≫ b) ↔ IsIso (𝟙 Y - b ≫ a). Their canonical inverse is inv (𝟙 X - a ≫ b) = 𝟙 X + a ≫ inv (𝟙 Y - b ≫ a) ≫ b under only IsIso (𝟙 Y - b ≫ a). The universes u, v are independent; no finiteness, biproduct or nonzero-object hypothesis is imposed. The rectangular unit-switch inverse below is a finite-matrix specialization. Criterion and inverse.
  • Rectangular unit switch. Over any Ring, for independently finite, decidable index types, a unit g = 1 + A * B determines a unit h = 1 + B * A with inverse 1 - B * g⁻¹ * A. Its diagonal pair diag(g, h⁻¹) is the ordered product U(A) L(B) U(-(g⁻¹ * A)) L(-(B * g)), hence elementary. Empty blocks and the zero ring are allowed; B * A = 0 recovers the zero-product stabilization commutator above. Construction and factorization, integer and boundary examples.
  • Ring Schur reduction. For independently finite, decidable block indices over any Ring, an invertible northwest block A makes [[A, B], [C, D]] a unit exactly when the ordered residual D - C * ⅟A * B is a unit. Given a unit residual, the corresponding block unit has an explicit ordered inverse; homomorphisms killing the elementary subgroup see only the diagonal pivot and residual, with no condition on the target group. Empty blocks and the zero ring are included. Construction and criterion, noncommutative and boundary clients.
  • Unit-pivot reduction and elementary diagonals. Over any CommRing, unit ordered leading minors permit two-sided elementary diagonalization; a product-one diagonal is elementary, and the unit-pivot, determinant-one criterion puts a finite GL unit in E. This is not a general SL = E theorem; the minor hypothesis is stronger than nonvanishing. More generally, if an elementary left factor can produce a unit leading entry for every invertible matrix at every successor rank, the same two-sided reduction exists at every finite rank. The latter theorem is conditional on that all-rank pivot property; it does not assert an unconditional elementary generation theorem. Unit-pivot guide, conditional pivot induction, diagonal guide.
  • Elementary generation over local rings. Every invertible finite matrix over a commutative local ring has elementary left and right factors reducing it to a diagonal of units, without a leading-minor hypothesis. At every finite rank, determinant one characterizes the elementary subgroup. The stable elementary subgroup is the determinant kernel, and the elementary quotient is canonically the coefficient units via the existing determinant and rank-one section. Over a field, Mathlib transvection lists give an invertible diagonal factorization. Local generation, nonfield and rank-boundary clients.
  • Elementary paths and SL neighborhoods. Over a topological ring, elementary GL units vary continuously with coefficients, even when the coefficient ring is noncommutative. Path-connected coefficients make the elementary subgroup path connected. For commutative coefficients, an open scalar-unit locus makes its comap in Mathlib's special linear group open and closed; additionally path-connected coefficients identify it with the identity path component. Paths guide, neighborhood guide.
  • Scalar unit reflection and canonical quasi-regular kernels. For f : R →+* S from a possibly noncommutative ring to a semiring, IsLocalHom f (reflection of units) makes the canonical two-sided kernel TwoSidedIdeal.ker f quasi-regular, without surjectivity, by RingHom.isQuasiregular_ker. For a surjective such map, unit reflection is equivalent to kernel quasi-regularity by RingHom.isLocalHom_iff_isQuasiregular_ker; its reverse direction is also available through RingHom.isLocalHom_of_surjective_of_isQuasiregular_ker. Mathlib supplies IsUnit (f a) ↔ IsUnit a via isUnit_map_iff; under surjectivity it also gives a surjective induced map on units. Source and target universes are independent; neither commutativity, local-ring structure, nontriviality nor nilness of the kernel is assumed. Nil kernels specialize through TwoSidedIdeal.IsNil.isQuasiregular.
  • Lifting modulo quasi-regular ideals. For a two-sided ideal, its unitization general linear group identifies with the congruence kernel; quasi-regularity of every ideal element additionally makes the quotient GL map surjective. The Jacobson radical is the greatest quasi-regular ideal, and pointwise nil ideals provide examples without a shared nilpotence exponent. Kernel equivalence, quotient lifting, ideal characterization.
  • Trace modulo additive commutators. For a chosen index p : n, trace gives an additive equivalence from square matrices modulo additive commutators to the coefficient ring modulo additive commutators; its inverse inserts a scalar in the p,p corner. The forward map also works at empty rank. These are additive groups, not quotient rings or a K₀ descent. Equivalence and forward map. The following characteristic laws use weaker hypotheses: cyclic representatives and unit conjugation require AddCommGroup with Mul, respectively Monoid, without distributivity; raw block trace requires only AddCommMonoid and two independently-universed finite index types, without decidable equality or nonemptiness. The raw Sum-indexed block shape complements mathlib's dependent trace_blockDiagonal' formula.
  • Finite elementary and relative structure. Off-diagonal elementary units generate E; ordered commutator laws prove that E is perfect at rank at least three. A two-sided ideal defines the relative normal closure inside E, not a claimed same-rank normal subgroup of ambient GL. Congruence units become relatively elementary after Whitehead doubling; stabilization preserves elementary, relative and congruence inclusions. Perfectness, relative subgroup, relative Whitehead, guides.
  • A Steinberg presentation and universal maps. Over any Ring, for an arbitrary index type, symbols satisfy precisely the additive, disjoint-root and ordered-composable-root relations, and compatible maps extend uniquely. Finite decidable indices additionally give a surjection onto E, not onto all of GL. The reverse relation has coefficient -(b * a); no opposite-root, central-kernel or K₂ theorem is claimed. Universal lift, onto-E map, presentation guide.
  • Units over square-zero kernels. For a homomorphism from any Ring to any Semiring, a square-zero kernel identifies the units mapping to 1 with the additive kernel, written multiplicatively. The coordinates are u ↦ u - 1 and x ↦ 1 + x, with inverse unit 1 - x. The hypothesis kills every mixed product of kernel elements; no commutativity or surjectivity is needed. The scalar dual-number equivalence is a specialization with its explicit lift and readback preserved. This is the algebraic ingredient of Fesenko–Vostokov, Chapter I (5.4)(3), not a unit-lifting or valuation-layer quotient theorem. Equivalence and coordinate API, dual-number comparison.
  • Principal-unit ideal layers on original-ring units. For left ideals I, J in any Ring, with I ^ 2 ≤ J, the stock stabilizer of [1] in the quotient left module R / I maps by u ↦ [u - 1] to the additive layer I / (I ∩ J), written multiplicatively. Its kernel is exactly the relative stabilizer modulo J, which is therefore normal. If additionally I ≤ Ring.jacobson R, the map is surjective and its kernel quotient is isomorphic to that additive layer, with inverse [x] ↦ [1 + x] in the original ring. Neither ideal needs to be two-sided, and J ≤ I is not assumed. For two-sided I, the carrier agrees with the kernel of reduction on units. The nonzero-denominator model uses (3)/(9) in ZMod 27; the integer boundary explains the original-unit lifting hypothesis. Equivalence and characteristic laws, models and boundary. For a commutative domain R, a nonzero principal ideal I = Ideal.span {π} contained in Ideal.jacobson ⊥, and n ≥ 1, the original-ring unit stabilizer at I ^ n, modulo its exact coordinate kernel (the relative deeper stabilizer at I ^ (n + 1)), is equivalent to Multiplicative (R ⧸ I). It sends [1 + a * π ^ n] to [a]; the inverse returns that original-ring unit class. The target is the additive coefficient ring, not necessarily a field. Replacing π by u * π for u : Rˣ with residue unit ū multiplies a fixed class's forward coefficient by (ū⁻¹) ^ n; the inverse at a fixed coefficient uses the old inverse with parameter multiplied by ū ^ n. Normality and surjectivity are conclusions, not assumptions; exponent zero instead gives multiplicative reduction. Coefficients and generator-change laws, coefficient clients.
  • Principal-unit commutators and nilpotence. Over any Ring R, including noncommutative and zero rings, write U(I) for the stock Rˣ-stabilizer of 1 in the quotient left module R ⧸ I. For two-sided I, this is the kernel of reduction on units to the quotient ring. Arbitrary left ideals A, B satisfy [U(A), U(B)] ≤ U(A * B + B * A). For two-sided I, [U(I ^ a), U(I ^ b)] ≤ U(I ^ (a + b)) for all a, b : ℕ, including zero, without a nilpotence assumption. For any left ideal I, the lower central series has LCS(k) ≤ U(I ^ (k + 1)), with zeroth term U(I). If I ^ N = ⊥, then U(I) is nilpotent of class at most N - 1 (natural subtraction); N = 0 and N = 1 give the trivial subgroup. This is not a nilpotence claim about the full unit group. Positive additive layers use the preceding ideal-layer and power-coordinate API, not an additive equivalence at exponent zero. Commutator bounds and nilpotence.
  • Images of principal-unit subgroups. For a ring homomorphism f : R →+* S with Ring R and Ring S in independent universes, and a left ideal I ≤ Ideal.jacobson ⊥, a unit u : Sˣ lies in the image under Units.map f.toMonoidHom of the original R-unit stabilizer of [1] in the quotient left module R ⧸ I exactly when ∃ a ∈ I, f a = (u : S) - 1. This uses the set image of ideal elements, not the extended ideal Ideal.map f I. The map need not be injective, and neither ring needs to be commutative; no domain, local-ring or valuation hypothesis is needed. Image membership criterion.
  • First-order dual-number kernels and conjugation. Over any CommRing, the multiplicative reduction identity fibers of Mathlib's GL and SL groups and of scalar units identify with additive matrices, trace-zero matrices and scalars. The determinant corresponds to trace; arbitrary ring-hom coefficient maps commute with these constructions without being implicitly R-linear. Unit left conjugation corresponds to g * X * g⁻¹, even for nonconstant dual-number units; the SL-kernel action uses SL conjugators. First-order fiber groups are abelian, so their commutator is not the generally nonzero matrix Lie bracket. Kernel equivalences, determinant/trace, adjoint guide.
  • Mixed dual-number GL and SL commutators. For finite matrices over a CommRing, units over iterated dual numbers satisfy the ordered identity [1 + εX, 1 + ηY] = 1 + εη(XY - YX) with full outer-kernel readback. When each input is trace zero, special-linear factors yield the analogous identity in Mathlib's SL group and trace-zero readback. The ε-factor reduces to the inner lift liftGL X (in SL, liftSL X), usually not 1: this is not a bracket of two elements of the abelian first-order outer kernel, nor a global Lie- or scheme-tangent theorem. GL identity, SL identity, GL guide, SL guide.
