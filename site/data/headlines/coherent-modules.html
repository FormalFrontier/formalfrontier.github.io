• Polynomial intertwining. For an arbitrary semiring S, additive commutative monoids M, N, and coefficient representations σ : S →+* AddMonoid.End M, τ : S →+* AddMonoid.End N, an additive map h : M →+ N that intertwines every σ s with τ s and the selected endomorphisms f with g also intertwines their plain polynomial evaluations. The semilinear version uses the given module structures over independent semirings R, R₂, any scalar homomorphism η : R →+* R₂, an η-semilinear map h, and representations into the respective linear endomorphism semirings. The hypotheses (τ s).comp h = h.comp (σ s) for every s and g.comp h = h.comp f imply (p.eval₂ τ g).comp h = h.comp (p.eval₂ σ f). All five carrier universes are independent. No coefficient/end commutation, finiteness, or module structure on the semilinear Hom is required.
  • Stable kernels and ranges. For an additive commutative group with its given module structure over a possibly zero, noncommutative semiring, simultaneous one-step stability at a natural bound, including zero, gives the specified sum equivalence from the power kernel and range. The kernel restriction is nilpotent and the range restriction is an automorphism. Canonical projections are independent of the stable bound and natural for arbitrary linear intertwiners. Noetherianity and Artinianity of the module give a common bound; the separate Noetherian-surjective and Artinian-injective automorphism criteria retain their asymmetric hypotheses. See StableKernelRange. Over arbitrary ring scalars, a finite-length module that is categorically indecomposable has a local endomorphism ring, and each endomorphism is nilpotent or a unit. The indecomposable predicate already excludes the zero module; see Local. The classical ring-module antecedents are Fitting's lemma and its indecomposable finite-length local-endomorphism consequence, as presented in Wisbauer, Foundations of Module and Ring Theory, §§31.13 and 32.4(3)(iii). The explicit-one-step semiring/additive-group decomposition above generalizes the cited ring-module setting; the local-endomorphism theorem retains ring scalars. See references and credit.
  • Internal Krull–Schmidt decompositions and cancellation. Over any ring, a Noetherian module or an Artinian module is a finite internal direct sum of nonzero indecomposable submodules; the two chain conditions are separate existence hypotheses. Each existence witness includes A i ≠ ⊥ alongside categorical indecomposability. Two supplied finite internal decompositions of linearly equivalent modules match up to an equivalence of index types and linear equivalences of summands when all summands have local endomorphism rings. Indecomposable summands and finite length of one total give the same matching. Both matching conclusions include ∀ i, A i ≠ ⊥ and ∀ j, B j ≠ ⊥, derived from the given hypotheses. Arbitrary finite index types use the caller's decidable equality instances and may be empty; existence uses the stock equality instance on Fin n. The matching is existential, not required to preserve the given coordinates. A common finite-length module P cancels from P × M ≃ₗ[R] P × N, with no chain condition on M or N; P may be zero. Zero modules, including those over a zero ring, have the empty decomposition. See KrullSchmidt, which realizes the categorical theorems of Homological Algebra through ModuleCat.
  • Additive intertwining by ranges. An additive map between commutative monoids intertwines a finite family of source endomorphisms summing to the identity with an orthogonal idempotent target family if and only if it maps each source range into its corresponding target range. No source orthogonality, target completeness, subtraction or cancellation is required, and the monoid and index universes are independent. The arbitrary-index range-action lemma also supplies the coordinate calculation for the existing linear product-of-ranges equivalence through Mathlib's canonical endomorphism action. See OrthogonalIdempotents.semiconj_iff_mapsTo_range and linearEquivPiRange.
  • Fibrewise additive intertwining. For additive commutative monoids M, N, let P : I → AddMonoid.End M have finite index set and sum to the identity, and let Q : J → AddMonoid.End N be an orthogonal idempotent family, with decidable equality on J. For any q : I → J and additive h : M →+ N, sending range (P i) into range (Q (q i)) for every i implies h ∘ (∑ i with q i = j, P i) = Q j ∘ h for every j. This implication uses no source orthogonality; it is an equivalence when P is a complete orthogonal idempotent family. The target label set may be infinite, and Q need not be complete; labels may be merged or omitted. An unused Q j need only kill h(M), not vanish on all of N. No subtraction, cancellation or scalar compatibility is required, and the two monoid and two index universes are independent. See OrthogonalIdempotents.semiconj_sum_fiberwise_of_mapsTo_range and CompleteOrthogonalIdempotents.semiconj_sum_fiberwise_iff_mapsTo_range.
  • Finite coarsening of complete orthogonal idempotents. Over any possibly noncommutative Semiring A, summing a finite complete orthogonal family over the fibres of an arbitrary map q : I → J gives a complete orthogonal family indexed by J. Both index types have Fintype instances, with decidable equality needed only on J; the three universes are independent. The map need not be surjective: unused labels have value zero. Empty index types and zero semirings are allowed; no additive inverses, cancellation or centrality is required. See CompleteOrthogonalIdempotents.sum_fiberwise.

  • Annihilator quotients with two scalar actions. Suppose a ring A and a semiring C act on the same additive commutative monoid M, with commuting given actions.
