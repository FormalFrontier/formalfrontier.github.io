Weighted unit derivative

Over an arbitrary commutative ring R, for a native unit f : (PowerSeries R)ˣ, negXLogDeriv f = -X * f' * f⁻¹, weighted by negative X. Products become sums, inverses become negatives and integer powers become integer multiples, naturally under coefficient-ring homomorphisms. Integral convolution works without normalization; a division-free forward recurrence for derivative coefficients requires constant coefficient one. Linear factors 1 - C a * X give signed power sums for finite products, including inverse factors and the empty family.

This is neither reconstruction nor injectivity: constant units and, under [CharP R p], p-th powers lie in the kernel; even normalized nonidentity units can do so. No formal logarithm, lambda/Witt structure, splitting extension or Adams operation is asserted. See the full formulas and limitations and source.

Rational Artin–Hasse exponential

The rational formal construction is defined for every natural p and has constant and linear coefficients one. For 2 ≤ p (composite p allowed), it is exp (∑ i ≥ 0, X^(p^i) / p^i); the coefficient recurrence together with a_0 = 1 determines it uniquely. The recurrence fails at p = 1, degree two.

For prime p only, its degree-k coefficient equals that of every finite Euler product of (1 - X^n)^(-μ(n)/n) over positive n ≤ N coprime to p, when k ≤ N. This is coefficientwise, not an infinite-product or analytic convergence assertion. Prime p also gives coefficient p-integrality (multiplicative p-adic valuation at most one), by an independent Dieudonné–Dwork proof using p-adic divided powers, not the Euler identity. The rational core does not import p-adic theory. See details and examples, the rational core, Euler product and separate p-adic module.

Ideal-adically restricted multivariate series

For any family q : ∀ i, C →+* Q i of homomorphisms between commutative semirings, MvPowerSeries.polynomialReductionSubalgebra σ q consists of series whose coefficientwise image under each q i is polynomial, equivalently has finite coefficient support. It uses the usual coefficient-C algebra and needs no compatibility, surjectivity, nonempty index type or finite variable type. Its polynomial inclusion and finite reductions are homomorphisms; a surjective row gives a surjective reduction. An empty family gives the whole series algebra, and one identity row gives precisely the polynomial subalgebra, with reduction inverse to inclusion.

For arbitrary variable types, commutative coefficient rings and ideals I, restrictedness requires that, at each k, only finitely many monomial indices have coefficients outside I ^ k. The restricted subring has surjective polynomial reductions, including k = 0, ideal-change maps, and residue maps for parameters in a local ring's maximal ideal. This subring and its reductions specialize the same construction to q k = Ideal.Quotient.mk (I ^ k).

  • Regrouping and kernels. First-variable regrouping is injective, not an equivalence with all iterated restricted series. Finite variables give a degree-cutoff criterion; with I.FG they also identify the intrinsic algebraic reduction kernel at every level. Regular principal ideals admit internal division and scalar-linear quotient equivalences. For arbitrary variables and ideals, coefficientwise reduction kernels multiply from levels n, k into level n + k, without completeness or finite generation; this does not identify intrinsic ideal powers or give an arbitrary-ideal intrinsic kernel identity.
  • Completion and units. For arbitrary R, I and σ, MvPolynomial.adicCompletionEquiv σ I identifies polynomial completion with the algebra of series over AdicCompletion I R that become polynomials under every original quotient evaluation. It uses the named AdicCompletion.extendedAlgebra on the source, not a new global instance. Under [IsAdicComplete I R], coefficient transport recovers the original ring equivalence with restricted series over R. Neither assertion assumes intrinsic completeness of arbitrary polynomial completions. Under coefficient completeness, a restricted series f is a unit exactly when adicReduction I 1 f is a unit, equivalently when its constant coefficient is a unit and every other coefficient lies in I.radical.
  • Coefficient and variable maps. For commutative rings R, T, ideals I, J, a homomorphism f : R →+* T, hf : I ≤ J.comap f, and any variable map g : σ → τ, the completed-coefficient series above admit a ring map MvPowerSeries.completedRestrictedMap I J f hf g. Infinite fibers are allowed: at each precision its coefficients are finite sums over the source polynomial reduction's support. Coefficient change is coefficientwise along AdicCompletion.mapRingHom I J f hf; renaming completes a finite direct-sum pushforward using the whole completion/restricted-product equivalence, independently of the polynomial-completion comparison. Identity, composition, polynomial and scalar laws hold, and MvPolynomial.adicCompletionEquiv commutes with these maps in both directions on all elements. No completeness, separation, finite generation or finite-variable hypothesis is required. See the map API.
  • Linear extension. Under [IsAdicComplete I R], every polynomial R-linear endomorphism extends to an R-linear restricted-series operator with finite-level equations. Uniqueness needs both agreement on polynomials and preservation of every coefficientwise reduction kernel; no ring-homomorphism extension or intrinsic restricted-ring completeness is asserted. See the extension guide.
  • Content, leading terms and division. Separated prevaluation rings give selected-coefficient content factorization. Over a valuation domain separated for the principal ideal of a parameter in its maximal ideal, intrinsic leading terms are polynomials over the actual ring R. Any two supplied divisions by a nonzero singleton divisor with actual-supported remainders agree; arbitrary-divisor existence is not asserted. Primitive-divisor existence and conditional ideal generation additionally require a nonzero parameter, radical equal to the maximal ideal, and adic completeness. Under those hypotheses, a finite primitive family in a restricted-series ideal generates it if its actual-R leading terms generate the full polynomial leading-term ideal. No standard-basis existence is asserted; conditional quotient-unit reflection does not construct a division.
  • First-variable preparation. Over a commutative valuation domain complete for the principal ideal of a nonzero parameter in its maximal ideal, with that ideal's radical equal to the maximal ideal, let a restricted series f in Fin (n + 1) variables have regrouped maximal-ideal residue polynomial of degree d with nonzero scalar top coefficient C c. There is a unique pair consisting of a restricted unit u and a monic first-variable polynomial G of exact degree d with polynomialRestrictedFinFirst _ n G = u * f. Actual-coefficient primitivity follows; uniqueness even allows competing multipliers not assumed units. n = 0 and d = 0 are included. See the preparation guide.
  • Generic cofinal support. For any R-module, I ≤ J, 1 ≤ N and J ^ N ≤ I equate separation, precompleteness and completeness for the two filtrations; this separately importable result needs no restricted series.
