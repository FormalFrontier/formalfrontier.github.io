• Galois and cyclic extensions from residue extensions. Let R be a Henselian valuation domain with fraction field F, and let a local commutative R-algebra S embed in a finite extension L/F. With compatible algebra and residue-field actions, suppose the residue degree equals [L : F]. If the residue extension is Galois, then L/F is Galois; if its algebra-automorphism group is also cyclic, so is that of L/F. A finite residue field of R suffices for both conclusions. See ValuationIntegers.Valuation.ResidueGalois.
  • Scalar multiples of units from independent reductions. Let R be a valuation domain with fraction field F, and let S be a local commutative R-algebra. With compatible algebra and residue-field actions, an F-basis of a ring L formed by images of elements of S whose reductions are linearly independent over the residue field of R expresses every x : L as a scalar times the image of a unit of S. The basis index may be arbitrary and S → L need not be injective. See Module.Basis.exists_isUnit_mul_of_linearIndependent_residue in ValuationIntegers.Valuation.ResidueUnits.

The valuation API below uses the dependencies credited in the references. The generic Henselian-pair estimates come from Adic Modules, positive-power comparison and polynomial-root inverse-image criteria from Group Theory, and the generic-point space from Spectral Stone Duality; their valuation applications are developed here.

  • Polynomial root-value bounds and inverse images (module, details). Let D,E be possibly noncommutative semirings, f : D →+* E, and ν : E →*₀ Γ satisfy the max-addition inequality, with Γ a linearly ordered commutative monoid with zero. For an eval₂ f root, source-supported coefficient values bounded by the nonzero mapped leading (respectively constant) value give ν x ≤ 1 (respectively 1 ≤ ν x). With coefficient values at most one, leading (respectively constant) value one gives the same bound, even if 0 = 1 in Γ; both give ν x = 1. No injectivity, coefficient commutation or nonzero root is assumed. Separately, Group Theory’s generic criteria show that any multiplication-only map μ : E → T to an arbitrary monoid sends a root to a left-invertible element if the mapped constant has a supplied left inverse; the root image is a unit iff it is right-regular. A left inverse alone is not a unit in general. Raw unit-constant roots have left inverses over semirings (hypotheses that force a ring structure); genuinely nonring evaluations are possible for the generic mapped theorem.
  • Finitely many distinct polynomial-root values (module, details). Under the same valuation-like semiring hypotheses, one source-supported coefficient of nonzero image value bounds the number of distinct root values, including zero, by the number of positive-degree supported coefficients of nonzero value, hence by p.natDegree. If A is the nonempty set of all such active coefficient indices, the nonzero root values number at most A.card - 1; add the zero-value occurrence for the total bound. Finiteness for nonzero polynomials over a division semiring also includes collapsed value targets. These count values, not roots or multiplicities.
  • Restriction, equivalence and canonical extensions (details and modules). On a division ring, valuations into independent linearly ordered commutative monoids with zero are equivalent iff their strict unit cuts agree, or iff their zero fibers and closed unit cuts agree. For a map of arbitrary rings whose range contains a positive power of every target element, restriction is injective for a fixed value monoid and reflects equivalence for different value monoids, without injectivity or surjectivity of the ring map. Purely inseparable field-algebra domains specialize this result. For fields F,E, if every x : E has a positive power in the scalar image of F, each valuation subring of F has a unique extension to E: its integral closure. Membership is integrality, or scalar membership after any specified positive power; purely inseparable field extensions supply the power witnesses.
  • Compatible valuation-ring extensions and prescribed residue fields (module). Along a field homomorphism, a chosen extension valuation subring restricts to the original valuation ring and preserves every value-order comparison on the base field, without identifying value groups. The original local coefficient map induces a commuting residue map. For any finite separable extension of the original residue field, a lifted primitive-generator polynomial gives a finite separable simple field extension of the same degree, an integral generator in its chosen valuation subring, and a base-compatible isomorphism from the given extension to its residue field. The selected power-basis generators correspond under reduction. No Henselianity, discreteness or completeness hypothesis is imposed.
  • Scalar-trivial values and valuation-separated linear independence (details and modules). In a ring algebra over a commutative ring, a valuation into a linearly ordered commutative monoid with zero that is trivial on nonzero scalars takes integral elements to zero or one. Over a field, scalar values at most one give the same conclusion for algebraic elements; a nonzero polynomial with all supported coefficients of value one suffices locally. For a field F, ring F-algebra A and valuation v, a family with nonzero values in pairwise distinct scalar-weighted value classes is linearly independent. In particular, v x ≠ 0 and separation of (v x)^n from every nonzero scalar value for 0 < n < d make the first d powers independent; if A is finite over F, then d ≤ Module.finrank F A. No domain on A or group structure on the value monoid is needed.
  • Residue degree bounded by field degree (module). Let R be a valuation domain with fraction field F, L/F a finite field extension, and S a commutative local R-algebra whose given algebraMap S L is injective, with commuting R/F/L and R/S/L scalar towers. Write κR, κS for the original residue fields. The given κR- and R-algebra structures on κS satisfy the R/κR/κS and R/S/κS towers; the residue square follows from these towers, rather than being an extra premise. ValuationRing.finite_residueField gives finite residue degree, and ValuationRing.finrank_residueField_le gives [κS : κR] ≤ [L : F]. Residue finiteness is a conclusion, not an input, and means finite degree, not finite cardinality. The domain structure on S and its flatness over R are derived from the embeddings and valuation-domain structure. No valuation-ring structure on S, fraction-field structure for S → L, or explicit local-map hypothesis on R → S is assumed. Universes are independent; no Henselianity, completeness, discreteness, separability, residue perfection or common value group is required.
  • Prescribed residue lifts give field and ring bases (module). Let R be a valuation domain with fraction field F, L/F a finite field extension, and S a commutative local R-algebra with its given injective algebraMap S L. Retain the original R/S/F/L algebras and actions, with commuting R/F/L and R/S/L scalar towers, and the given κR- and R-algebra structures on κS, with both R/κR/κS and R/S/κS towers. These two residue towers imply the residue square and localness of the original R → S map; neither is an extra premise. Given a κR-basis b : ι → κS indexed by a finite type, prescribed lifts s : ι → S, and reduction equations residue S (s i) = b i, assume card ι = [L : F]. ValuationRing.basisOfResidue constructs an F-basis of L with vectors algebraMap S L (s i), and ValuationRing.integralBasisOfResidue constructs an R-basis of S with vectors s i, for the given actions and maps. An element of L lies in the image of S → L iff every field coordinate lies in the image of R → F; it lies in the image of the maximal ideal of S iff every coordinate lies in the image of the maximal ideal of R. For finite sums ∑ i, a i • algebraMap S L (s i), the same range and maximal-ideal criteria test the coefficients a i directly. These two criteria need neither finite ambient dimension nor card ι = [L : F], while retaining the finite index, given residue basis, prescribed lifts and reductions, and all original maps, towers and injectivity of S → L. Ring and field coordinates agree under R → F, and ring coordinates reduce to the b-coordinates of the residue. Both bases are uniquely characterized by their prescribed vectors. Membership in the maximal ideal of S is coefficientwise membership in the maximal ideal of R; S is finite and free as an R-module. No valuation-ring structure on S, fraction-field structure for S → L, or prior finiteness, freeness, flatness or integrality of S over R is assumed. The domain structure on S and its flatness over R are derived. Universes are independent; no Henselianity, completeness, discreteness, separability, residue perfection, finite residue-field cardinality or common value group is required. This is degree exhaustion by the residue-basis size, not a definition of an unramified extension.
  • Trace and norm for prescribed residue lifts (module). Under the preceding prescribed-lift hypotheses, including finite L/F and card ι = [L : F], ordinary algebraic trace and norm commute with the original embeddings R → F and S → L, and with the canonical residue maps. Finiteness and freeness of S over R follow from the lifted ring basis; neither is an extra premise. Residue separability is not required, and there is no restriction on characteristic.
  • Trace-one units and trace surjectivity (module). Let R be a valuation domain with fraction field F, L/F a finite field extension, and S a commutative local R-algebra with its given injective S → L. Retain the original ambient and residue algebra actions and the four compatible scalar towers described above. If [κS : κR] = [L : F] and the given residue extension κS/κR is separable, ValuationRing.exists_unit_trace_eq_one gives a unit of the original S whose original R-linear trace is one, and ValuationRing.trace_surjective makes Algebra.trace R S : S →ₗ[R] R surjective onto all of R. Residue bases, lifts and finite freeness are internal, not caller premises. No Henselianity, finite residue-field cardinality, invertible degree, valuation-ring structure on S, or fraction-field structure for S → L is required.
  • Valuation of a norm under residue-degree exhaustion (module). Let L/F be a finite field extension with given valuations v, w and original integer rings R, S. Retain the compatible ambient and residue algebra structures and scalar towers above, and assume [L : F] = [κS : κR]. For any monoid-with-zero homomorphism j between their independent value groups satisfying w (algebraMap F L a) = j (v a) for every a : F, Valuation.map_norm_eq_pow_of_finrank_eq_residueField gives j (v (Algebra.norm F x)) = (w x) ^ Module.finrank F L for every x : L. No injectivity or order preservation of j, Henselianity, separability, discreteness or finite residue-field cardinality is assumed.
  • Unit norms over a Henselian valuation domain (principal congruences, finite residue fields). In the preceding compatible valuation-domain and local embedded-algebra setting, assume R Henselian, [L : F] = [κS : κR], and the given residue extension separable. For every ideal I ≤ maximalIdeal R and u : R with u - 1 ∈ I, there is a unit s : S with s - 1 ∈ I.map (algebraMap R S) and Algebra.norm R s = u. No primitive element, basis, finite/free module or trace-unit witness is a premise. For every such ideal I, ValuationRing.map_norm_units_ker_quotient identifies the image under the unit norm of the kernel of reduction modulo I.map (algebraMap R S) with the kernel of reduction modulo I. Its positive-power specialization maps the kernel modulo maximalIdeal S ^ n onto the corresponding kernel for R, for every n > 0. No Galois, prime-degree, invertible-degree, discreteness or completeness hypothesis is required. If the base residue field is finite, every unit of R is the norm of a unit of the original S, and the kernel-image equality holds for every n, including zero. Residue separability is derived from finiteness, rather than assumed. This combines finite-field unit norm surjectivity, the residue norm comparison and the principal-congruence correction.
  • The field norm group (module). In the preceding compatible valuation-domain and local embedded-algebra setting, assume R Henselian with finite residue field and [L : F] = [κS : κR]. ValuationRing.mem_range_norm_units_iff identifies the usual norm subgroup (Units.map (Algebra.norm F : L →* F)).range of Fˣ: its elements are exactly a ^ [L : F] * image(u) for a : Fˣ and u : Rˣ, using the given R → F map. Residue bases and lifts are chosen internally. No valuation-ring structure on S, fraction-field structure for S → L, separability or degree invertibility is assumed. This is the norm on Lˣ, not the zero-inclusive range on L.
  • Power bases and minimal polynomials from arbitrary primitive residue lifts (power bases, polynomial comparisons, derivatives and separability). In the preceding valuation-domain, local embedded-algebra setting, assume [L : F] = [κS : κR]. For every α : κS with Algebra.adjoin κR {α} = ⊤ and every θ : S reducing to α, ValuationRing.integralPowerBasisOfPrimitiveResidue constructs a power basis of the original S/R, and ValuationRing.powerBasisOfPrimitiveResidue constructs a power basis of the original L/F with generator algebraMap S L θ. Both dimensions are natDegree (minpoly κR α), equal to the field and residue degrees. Their full bases are the prescribed-lift bases above, formed from the stock residue power basis and the same family θ ^ i, without reindexing. The stock power-basis generation theorem gives S = R[θ] and L = F[algebraMap S L θ]. For the integral power basis pbR, the monic polynomial pbR.minpolyGen = minpoly R θ maps under R → F exactly to minpoly F (algebraMap S L θ) and reduces under R → κR exactly to minpoly κR α. These are coefficientwise equalities, not just association of polynomials. Residue finiteness is derived; no power basis, finite R-module structure or integrality of S is assumed. Neither comparison requires separability. No Henselianity, discreteness, completeness or common value group is required. If minpoly κR α is separable, then pbR.minpolyGen.derivative evaluates at the same prescribed θ to a unit in the original S, and the original extension L/F is separable. Under this separability hypothesis, for a commutative local R-algebra T with the given R/κR/κT and R/T/κT residue towers and a value hT : HenselianLocalRing T, every κR-algebra homomorphism φ : κS →ₐ[κR] κT lifts uniquely to an R-algebra homomorphism S → T whose reduction agrees with φ on every element of S (residue homomorphisms).
  • Unique residue and field homomorphism lifts (lifting, identity and composition). Let R be a Henselian valuation domain with fraction field F. Let S and T be commutative local R-algebras embedded by their given algebra maps in finite field extensions L/F and E/F, respectively, with the compatible ambient and residue scalar towers above. Assume [L : F] = [κS : κR], [E : F] = [κT : κR], and explicitly [Algebra.IsSeparable κR κS]. ValuationRing.existsUnique_residueAlgHom_lift_of_finrank_eq gives, for every φ : κS →ₐ[κR] κT, a unique ψ : S →ₐ[R] T satisfying residue T (ψ s) = φ (residue S s) for every s : S. ValuationRing.existsUnique_fieldAlgHom_lift_of_finrank_eq also gives a unique ι : L →ₐ[F] E admitting such a ψ with algebraMap T E (ψ s) = ι (algebraMap S L s) for every s : S. The identity and composition laws follow from this characterization. For composition of given compatible witnesses, the intermediate ambient algebra need only be a commutative ring, without intermediate separability or finite-degree assumptions; the source and final target retain the lifting hypotheses. A finite separable source residue extension supplies a primitive generator and its lift. The target degree equality gives module finiteness, integrality of the given R → T map, and Henselianity of T from that of R. No Module.Finite R T, target Henselianity, target separability or freeness assumption is required; the domain property of T follows from its embedding into E, rather than from an additional domain typeclass. The six universes are independent, and the given maps, actions and towers are retained. Both degree equalities and source residue separability are restrictions; this is not a statement for arbitrary finite ambient extensions or a full unramified-extension equivalence.
  • Absolute values and rank-at-most-one realizations (generic layer, rank and topology details). Nonarchimedean real absolute values on nontrivial rings and zero-reflecting NNReal-valued valuations on rings convert in both directions with round-trip laws. Pullbacks and rational restriction are included. Unit bounds for division semirings and bounded-range criteria for real absolute values are also provided. On a division ring, rank-at-most-one valuation data (with a linearly ordered commutative value group with zero) give a real absolute value reflecting the preorder and unit ball, with an ultrametric uniform equivalence WithVal v ≃ᵤ WithAbs v.absoluteValue. Trivial valuations are included; rank one gives nontriviality and, on valued fields, Mathlib's Valued.toNormedField norm.
  • Scalar and binomial valuation formulas (details and modules). Multiplication-preserving, nonnegative nonarchimedean maps on a nonassociative ring satisfy natural prime-power formulas at a prime p with f p < f 1, retaining the factor f 1. The target needs MulOneClass, natural powers with NatPowAssoc, zero, a linear order and two-sided positive-multiplication monotonicity, not preservation of zero or one by the map. For valuations on arbitrary rings into linearly ordered commutative monoids with zero, prime-binomial and prime-power upper bounds hold without integrality or cancellation. If p is prime, v p < 1 and both v (x-1)^(p-1), v (z-1)^(p-1) < v p, then v (x^m-z^m) = v m * v (x-z) for all natural m; for units it holds for all integer powers as well. No commutation is needed, and zero distances are included. Natural powers with v m = 1 preserve distances in the strict unit ball about one.
  • Rank, dimension, normalization and bounded modules (details and modules). For a field valuation into a linearly ordered commutative group with zero and a commutative local integer presentation hv : val.Integers O, rank at most one gives positive-power divisibility y ∣ x^n for maximal-ideal x and nonzero y, and Ring.KrullDimLE 1 O. Conversely that dimension bound gives rank-at-most-one data. Rank concerns the restricted value group; dimension zero and trivial valuations are included. Surjective valuations on any ring into a common linearly ordered commutative group with zero with cyclic units are equivalent iff equal. Integer presentations and DVR valuations have comparison laws. A bounded submodule of a finite-dimensional normed space over complete K is finitely generated over commutative Noetherian R with compatible actions whose image contains K's closed unit ball. For a finite-dimensional normed K-algebra that is a normed ring, v.HasExtension w, Noetherian base integers, a v-integral base norm unit ball and norm-at-most-one w-integers give finite extension-integer modules.
  • Valuative topologies, Cauchy filters and completions (topology and Cauchy details, completion details). Here value groups are arbitrary linearly ordered commutative groups with zero. Powers tend to zero iff x <ᵥ 1 in rank-at-most-one valuative rings; on division rings, the identity homeomorphism recognizes equivalence of two such valuations. Supplied integer-valued uniformities have integer-threshold Cauchy, limit and completeness criteria. In any valuative additive uniformity on a ring, Cauchy filters have bounded compatible valuation values and either converge to a fixed center or stabilize at a positive difference value. A supplied valued ring is complete iff its inherited integer subring is complete, at arbitrary rank. A compatible field valuation extends to its given uniform completion with the same value group and induces its topology. A dense exactly value-preserving ring homomorphism from a valued field to a complete separated valued ring identifies that ring with the completion. Dense HasExtension field algebra maps induce dense integer maps and bijective residue maps, also for different value groups; integer surjectivity is not asserted.
  • Finite intersections, approximation and spectra (details and modules). Arbitrary families of valuations on a field into a common linearly ordered commutative group with zero have intersection integers and full residue maps. Finite pointwise rank-at-most-one families have denominator, localization and fraction-field results, residue surjectivity at each selected nontrivial place, dimension at most one and finite spectra whose opens are empty or contain zero. Every maximal ideal is contracted for nonempty finite families, even without rank assumptions. Finite pairwise inequivalent rank-one families have weak approximation at positive restricted-value radii, residue-product surjectivity and a spectrum homeomorphism with Topology.WithGenericPoint. Diagonal uniformizers additionally require discreteness; repeated or trivial places need the separate boundaries below.
  • Laurent-series integers and approximation (details and modules). Over a field K, maximal-ideal powers in the standard Laurent-series valuation subring are detected by vanishing coefficients at natural indices below the power; units are exactly those with nonzero zeroth coefficient, and maximal-ideal elements exactly those with zero zeroth coefficient. The residue field is K, with reduction given by that coefficient. Laurent series over a type with zero have finite support below every integer cutoff; over a semiring a Laurent polynomial matches those coefficients, and over a field these polynomials are dense.
  • Henselian roots with sharp valuation precision (details and modules). For a field valuation into a linearly ordered commutative group with zero whose integers O are Henselian at their maximal ideal 𝔪, v(f(a)) < v(f′(a))² for f : O[X], a : O, gives a root with exact error identity, derivative-value stability and uniqueness in the strict derivative ball. For natural s, the conditions f(a) ∈ 𝔪^(2*s+1) and f′(a) ∉ 𝔪^(s+1) give correction in 𝔪^(s+1). Applied to powers, v(a-1) < v(m : K)² gives b^m = a, v b = 1 and exact correction value; nonzero exponent casts give open power ranges in Kˣ. No completeness, rank, discreteness, monicity or unit-derivative assumption is required.
  • Bernoulli integrality and complete denominators (details and modules). For every integer n and positive natural k, n^k*(n^k-1)*B_k/k is integral over ℤ, including B₁ = -1/2. For the divided Bernoulli number B_(2*k)/(2*k) with positive natural k, selected primes p satisfying p-1 ∣ 2*k give additive p-adic valuation -1-padicValNat p (2*k) and reduced-denominator exponent 1+padicValNat p (2*k). The complete-denominator API gives zero factorization coordinates otherwise and expresses that same reduced denominator as a finite prime product. The product formula also includes k = 0, when the denominator and product are one. A separate rational-integrality criterion bounds prime denominator support. For an integer-valued valuation on any ring, an element with a q^n-th root at every depth, for natural q > 1, has value zero or one; compatible roots are not required.
