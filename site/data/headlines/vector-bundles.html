• Semiring kernel projections and splitting compatibility. The equivalence ContinuousLinearMap.equivProdKerOfRightInverse is provided by ProjectiveModules. This library extracts its continuous kernel projection and preserves its existing kernel-product API by transparent reuse. Prefer the canonical ProjectiveModules equivalence and its coordinate and Ring-agreement theorems; the corresponding VectorBundles names remain as deprecated compatibility API. The extracted kernel projection is not deprecated. For a semiring R, an additive commutative group E with a topological additive-group structure, and an additive commutative monoid F with a topology, a continuous R-linear map f : E →L[R] F with a given continuous linear right inverse s gives E ≃L[R] F × f.ker, with coordinates (f x, x - s (f x)) and inverse (y, k) ↦ s y + k (module). When R is a ring and F an additive group, both bundled constructions agree with Mathlib's on the same data. No scalar topology, separation or completeness is assumed.
  • In a normed ring finite dimensional over an arbitrary field, every element a with ‖1 - a‖ < 1 is a unit, and the unit locus is open. No scalar norm or completeness is required. The same conclusions hold in left-Artinian normed rings (module). A pointwise-continuous family of continuous endomorphisms of a finite-dimensional normed space consequently has an open invertible locus, even over an incomplete nontrivially normed field (module).
  • Over a possibly incomplete nontrivially normed field, a family of existing continuous linear maps between given seminormed norm-compatible vector spaces, with finite-dimensional original domain, is continuous in the original operator-norm topology exactly when every original fixed-vector evaluation is continuous (ContinuousLinearMap.continuous_iff_apply_of_finiteDimensional in module). The canonical identity to the pointwise-convergence copy of the same continuous linear maps is a continuous linear equivalence (ContinuousLinearEquiv.toPointwiseConvergenceCLMOfFiniteDimensional); convergence along any filter is therefore equivalent to convergence at every fixed vector (ContinuousLinearMap.tendsto_iff_apply_of_finiteDimensional). For an isometric field homomorphism σ : K →+* L, the same criterion holds for continuous σ-semilinear maps with their original actions and seminorms (ContinuousLinearMap.continuous_iff_apply_of_finiteDimensionalₛₗ). Here K is nontrivially normed and L need only be a normed field. More generally, the ambient domain need not be finite-dimensional: evaluations on a set D suffice when its original K-span is finite-dimensional and dense (ContinuousLinearMap.continuous_iff_apply_of_dense_spanₛₗ); D need not be bounded. For any family of bounded sets, the canonical identity to uniform convergence on that family is a continuous L-linear equivalence if D lies in the K-span of its union (ContinuousLinearEquiv.toUniformConvergenceCLMOfDenseTestSpan in module). Only the dense controlling span of D must be finite-dimensional, so further bounded sets may be added even when they make the whole union span infinite-dimensional. The union-based construction (ContinuousLinearEquiv.toUniformConvergenceCLMOfDenseSpan) is the D = ⋃₀𝔖 specialization, and the algebraic-spanning finite-dimensional construction (ContinuousLinearEquiv.toUniformConvergenceCLMOfFiniteDimensional) specializes it further. No completeness, separation or surjectivity of σ is required. For example, R over Q has the one-dimensional dense test span Q·1, although its ambient algebraic dimension is infinite: D = {1} controls the family consisting of {1} and [-1,1], whose union spans all of R over Q. In Q(√2) with inherited real absolute value, Q·1 is proper and dense. The equivalence acts on the same full maps, not on their restrictions; dense restriction need not be injective for a nonseparated target.
  • Pointwise total boundedness, compactness and properness. Let σ : K →+* L be isometric, with K nontrivially normed and L a normed field. Let A and G be seminormed spaces over K and L, and let P be a dense finite-dimensional K-submodule of A. A family in the full space A →SL[σ] G is totally bounded exactly when every evaluation image on P is totally bounded (ContinuousLinearMap.totallyBounded_iff_apply_of_dense_finiteDimensionalₛₗ). If G is complete, a closed family is compact exactly under this pointwise condition (ContinuousLinearMap.isCompact_iff_forall_totallyBounded_image_apply_of_isClosed_of_dense). For proper G, the evaluation images need only be metrically bounded (ContinuousLinearMap.isCompact_iff_forall_isBounded_image_apply_of_isClosed_of_dense), and the full map space is proper for its canonical operator seminorm (ContinuousLinearMap.properSpace_of_dense_finiteDimensionalₛₗ in module). The ambient domain need not be finite-dimensional. No completeness of either field or of A, separation, or surjectivity of σ is required.
  • Projective isometry sections. A surjective linear isometry onto a projective module over a semiring has a chosen linear-isometric right inverse (LinearIsometry.sectionOfSurjective in module). It is a uniform embedding even for nonseparated seminormed modules; no scalar topology or completeness is required. For an originally normed source it agrees with the inverse of Mathlib's surjective-isometry equivalence. The separation quotient therefore has a linear-isometric section when its module is projective, using the given continuous constant scalar actions.
  • Isometric kernel-product decompositions. Over any semiring, a linear isometry f : E →ₗᵢ[R] F of seminormed additive groups with a linear right inverse s gives E ≃ₗᵢ[R] (F × f.toLinearMap.ker) (LinearIsometry.equivProdKerOfRightInverse in module). The kernel keeps its original module and inherited geometry; the product uses the maximum norm. The kernel may be nonzero. No separation, scalar norm or topology, or continuity assumption on s is required. The whole underlying linear and continuous equivalences agree with the canonical continuous splitting, including its ring form.

These families are proved here, with the original fiber and total-space topologies. The catalogue gives the individual statements and variants; the detailed guide adds examples and distinguishes Mathlib constructions.

  • Nearby noncommuting idempotents. For bounded idempotents P and Q on a normed space over a nontrivially normed field, finite-dimensional range of P and ‖Q - P‖ < 1 give continuous linear equivalences between their ranges and between their kernels. The compression and kernel-coordinate constructions use the original subspace norms; neither the field nor the ambient space needs to be complete. Finite dimension of the range of Q is a consequence, not an extra hypothesis. For a continuous family of idempotents over an arbitrary topological base, the kernel graph on the norm neighborhood has product coordinates preserving the base point. A kernel section is continuous exactly when its fixed-kernel coordinate is continuous.
  • Supported weighted coordinates. Given module families over a commutative semiring and linear chart equivalences on arbitrary subsets, the weighted coordinate map takes values in the full dependent product of model modules. Its membership, nonmembership, zero-weight and chart-evaluation rules are explicit. At each point, generating the unit ideal by the valid chart weights suffices for fibrewise injectivity. In particular, one valid unit weight suffices; no individual weight need be a unit in the general criterion. On the original equality fibres of a topological projection, with given module operations and inherited topology, the total coordinate map is continuous when the projection, weights and exact inverse coordinates are continuous, scalar multiplication on each model is continuous, and the closure of each weight's cozero set lies in its domain. No openness, finite-index, finite-dimensionality, norm or continuity of fibre operations is required.
  • Additive sum–kernel coordinates. For a projection p : T → B with given additive monoid structures on its equality fibres, additive maps β b into a topological abelian group A and retractions ρ b give a homeomorphism from {z : T × A // ρ (p z.1) z.2 = 0} to B × A when their two total maps are continuous. Its coordinates are (p t, β (p t) ⟨t,rfl⟩ + v); its inverse uses the given retraction and residual a - β b (ρ b a). Continuity into the original subspace is equivalent to continuity of the base and sum coordinates. No continuity of fibre operations or scalar topology is required. Given semiring-linear maps specialize this scalar-free construction by forgetting linearity, retaining their original scalar actions. For semiring-linear split maps on a given family E b, the complementary-range sum coordinates identify the fiberwise product of E b and range (id - β.comp ρ) with B × A, under the same two total continuity hypotheses. The source has the canonical product-total topology from the given first total space and the range graph topology. The inverse uses ρ and the complementary endomorphism, and the forward fiber map is the linear coproduct. No separate kernel topology is assumed.
  • Prescribed finite-dimensional projection families. Over a nontrivially normed field K, let the original finite-dimensional normed ambient A have its module topology ([IsModuleTopology K A]). Linear encoders and retractions between the equality fibres of p : T → B and A, with ρ b (β b u) = u, yield an operator-norm-continuous family of idempotents acting by β b (ρ b a) when the two exact total maps are continuous. The complete-field frontend obtains this module topology locally from finite dimensionality; the core construction needs no scalar completeness. The existing complementary continuous-map expression acts by a - β b (ρ b a), lies in the retraction kernel and vanishes on encoded vectors; it gives the image–kernel decomposition of every ambient vector. No fibre norm or continuity of fibre operations, ambient completeness, nontriviality of A, or separation of the base is assumed. Neither orthogonality nor commutation at different parameters is asserted; the ambient norm stays unchanged, including the standard sup norm for finite Pi.
  • Joint evaluation of algebraic semilinear families. For possibly noncommutative semirings R,S, a ring homomorphism σ : R →+* S and a finite R-basis of the source, assume that its scalar coordinates composed with σ are continuous. Joint continuity of a family of σ-semilinear maps is then equivalent to continuity at each basis vector. Only S, the source, target and parameter carry topologies; the target needs continuous addition and jointly continuous S-scalar multiplication. No continuity of source operations or of σ is assumed. The linear criterion is the identity specialization. Over a complete nontrivially normed field, a finite-dimensional Hausdorff topological source module needs no explicit basis data: joint continuity is equivalent to continuity at every fixed vector. For continuous linear maps from a finite-dimensional seminormed domain to a seminormed target, joint continuity is equivalent to continuity in the given operator-norm topology even over an incomplete field; neither space needs to be separated or complete. See JointEvaluation.
  • Total-space continuity of semilinear maps from finite local bases. Let p : T → X be continuous, with given R-module structures and subspace topologies on its equality-subtype fibres, and let σ : R →+* S be a homomorphism of semirings, with a topology on S. Coordinate models with finite bases may vary over an arbitrary open cover. If their scalar basis coordinates composed with σ and exact inverse vector coordinates are continuous, parameter-continuity of the images of the basis vectors gives continuity of the total semilinear fibre map into any S-module with continuous addition and scalar multiplication. No topology on R, continuity of σ, continuous operations on the models or fibres, injectivity, forward-realization continuity, norm, completeness or separation is required. See LinearMap.continuous_total_of_local_coordinates_of_basis_semilinear. The linear criterion LinearMap.continuous_total_of_local_coordinates_of_basis is the identity-hom specialization. Over a complete nontrivially normed field, finite-dimensional Hausdorff topological coordinate models supply the bases and scalar-coordinate continuity; fixed-vector frame continuity gives the corollary LinearMap.continuous_total_of_local_coordinates.
  • Module topologies on retracts and finite-basis coordinates. Over a semiring with a given scalar topology, a continuous linear retract of a module with the module topology inherits that topology, even when the continuous section is nonlinear. In particular, the range of an algebraic idempotent of such a module has its module topology as the inherited subspace topology. Over a topological semiring, a supplied finite basis of a module with the module topology gives a continuous linear equivalence with the standard finite scalar product; neither a field, a norm, completeness nor separation is required. See module-topology descent and finite-basis coordinates. The complete-field comparison agrees exactly with Mathlib's Module.Basis.equivFunL without assuming a source norm.
  • Local constancy of fibre finrank. Over a division ring, given pointwise linear equivalences to fixed models on sets whose interiors cover the base make natural-number fibre finrank locally constant. Neither finite-dimensionality nor topology on the modules is assumed. Infinite-dimensional modules have finrank zero; this is not a claim about their cardinal dimension.
  • Closed fiber embeddings. For continuous p : T → X, give its equality-subtype fibers their subspace topologies and RCLike module structures. An arbitrary open cover with finite-dimensional normed linear coordinates, jointly continuous realizations and continuous exact inverse-coordinate maps on the total-space restrictions gives a closed embedding into X × A from supplied injective algebraic fiber maps whose local frames are parameter-continuous at each fixed vector. The normed inner-product target need not be complete; local ranks may vary. The inverse extends to a jointly continuous fiberwise linear retraction on the product.
  • Finite Euclidean embeddings from weighted local coordinates. For a continuous map p : T → X over K = ℝ or ℂ, finitely many normed finite-dimensional local models for the original equality-subtype fibre modules give a closed embedding T → X × E into a finite Euclidean product. The supplied domains need not be open: their forward realizations and exact inverse coordinates are continuous on the given subspaces, and the continuous scalar weights have no common zero and closed cozero supports contained in their specified domains. The nonzero-weight sets provide an open cover. The second coordinate is the weighted coordinate map followed by the inverse of a linear equivalence from E to the full product of models. That product keeps its supremum norm; no isometry is asserted. The inverse on the image extends to a jointly continuous fibrewise linear retraction on all of X × E, preserving the given fibre operations and inherited topologies. No positivity, normalization, or separation of X is required. See exists_retraction_isClosedEmbedding_of_weighted_local_coordinates.
  • Retractions and projection continuity. Over real or complex scalars, original fibers with inducing realizations, jointly continuous local realizations on an open cover, finite-dimensional seminormed coordinate models and injective algebraic fiber maps into a normed inner-product space with fixed-vector-continuous local frames admit continuous-linear retractions, jointly continuous realizations and operator-norm-continuous orthogonal projections. No fiber norms or ambient completeness are needed. Separately, a given self-adjoint idempotent family is continuous if its images have local injective finite-dimensional algebraic frames with fixed-vector continuity; those frame domains need no topology.
  • Metrics and finite complements. A finite-dimensional real or complex bundle over a paracompact Hausdorff base admits a continuous positive-definite Hermitian metric inducing its original fiber topologies. Over a compact normal base, a finite-dimensional RCLike bundle has a finite-dimensional complement whose product is linearly trivial, not necessarily a complement to a prescribed embedding.
  • Orthogonal splitting. For a supplied continuous Hermitian metric, an intrinsic submodule bundle with embedded total-space inclusion and a finite-dimensional RCLike ambient model, orthogonal addition is a homeomorphism with continuous projections, over any base. Comparison identifies complements for two supplied metrics with their induced topologies; no metric is constructed.
  • Given adjoint partners. For RCLike inner products, a supplied partner S : A → H bundles linearly when H is normed and A seminormed; a pointwise norm bound on the first map makes it continuous-linear. A continuous-linear first map supplies the bound, without completeness. The catalogue also gives the algebraic semiring-valued pairing result; the partner is supplied, not constructed.
  • Adjoints and Gram families. A continuous linear map from a complete normed RCLike inner-product space to a seminormed one has an adjoint. For given raw adjoint partners between seminormed spaces, operator-norm continuity of the forward family gives joint continuity of the partners. On a finite-dimensional normed domain with normed ambient space, injective forward operators and given continuous-linear adjoint partners have continuous inverse Gram composites when the forward family is operator-norm continuous. On a complete finite-dimensional normed inner-product domain, a jointly continuous injective family instead constructs canonical adjoints, left inverses and projections continuously. None of these results requires completeness of the ambient space.
  • Algebraic orthogonal retractions and distance. An injective linear map from a plain module into a normed RCLike inner-product space has an algebraic retraction when its range has orthogonal projection; finite dimension of the module suffices. Given the orthogonal-retraction laws, best-approximation and squared-error formulas hold without completeness or a topology on the coefficient module. In a seminormed ambient space, zero residual norm detects range closure; in a normed one, β.range = r.kerᗮ and minimizers are unique. The catalogue also gives the nonzero-idempotent norm bound.
  • Finite orthogonal idempotent families. Over any semiring and finite index type, the matched-product sum intertwines two orthogonal idempotent families. If it is a unit, that same unit simultaneously conjugates them and forces both families to sum to one; completeness of the families is a conclusion, not a hypothesis.
  • Close idempotents and rank stability. On a normed space over a nontrivially normed field, idempotents at operator-norm distance less than one have equal cardinal ranks of ranges and kernels; these ranks are locally constant in continuous families, without completeness. When geometric series are summable in the endomorphism ring (in particular for a complete space), one normalized unit gives continuous-linear equivalences of both.
  • Fixed-kernel coordinates. For seminormed spaces over a nontrivially normed field, a family Γ d : H →L[K] A with supplied algebraic retractions r d has product coordinates for its total kernel on any topological parameter domain where q₀ ∘ Γ d is a unit. Here q₀ is fixed and continuous-linear; joint continuity of the difference between the original and normalized ambient projections suffices. No separate continuity of r or completeness is needed. The catalogue gives the formulas, unit-subtype version and distinction between a unit comparison and a retraction.
  • Linear families and bundle inverses. Over a field with a T₁ topology making it a topological ring, pointwise-continuous algebraic linear families have an open surjectivity locus for a finite-dimensional target with its module topology; the source needs no topology. Over a nontrivially normed field, continuous-forward fiberwise linear equivalences give bundle homeomorphisms when the source normed model is finite-dimensional and the target normed model has its module topology.
  • Compact refinements and partitions of unity. An ambient open cover of two compact subsets' product admits finite ambient open grids with each full rectangle in a chosen cover member, without separation assumptions. Closed-interval subdivisions allow repeated cuts and singleton intervals. A finite open cover of a normal space admits continuous weights in [0,1] summing to one, with closed cozero supports subordinate to the cover; no T₁ hypothesis is needed. On a closed subset of a normal space, a finite open cover similarly admits nonnegative continuous weights with closed supports in its members, sum at most one everywhere and exactly one on that subset.
  • Finite selection from supplied product charts. For compact X, arbitrary topological Y and chosen y₀, supplied whole-product charts with both base laws and agreement in both directions with specified original charts at y₀ admit a finite ordered open cover. This retains varying models and, when provided, matching linear coordinates over any semiring. It selects supplied charts, not charts from an arbitrary atlas over Y, and needs neither compactness of Y nor projection continuity.
  • Heterogeneous cylinder gluing. For a continuous projection over X × T, with T linearly ordered and topological with closed upper and lower rays, supplied base-preserving charts on the closed half-cylinders may have different models. The gluer gives a whole-cylinder homeomorphism into the left model, retaining its entire left chart.
  • Whole-cylinder charts and transport. An open base-preserving atlas for a continuous projection over X × unitInterval gives local whole-interval charts, with linear coordinates when the input coordinates are linear, over any semiring. Separately, finite ordered whole-cylinder charts with continuous weights in [0,1] summing to one and closed cozero supports inside the chart base sets give base-preserving, bottom-fixing transport from the standard pullback of the entire zero slice. A finite open cylinder cover on a normal base supplies the weights. Models may vary; full homotopy invariance is not asserted.
  • Varying-rank atlases. Over a field with a given topology, a supplied finite-rank linear atlas allows unbounded, locally varying ranks, including zero, on the original fibers and total space. Its projection is continuous, open and quotient, and its zero section is an embedding. Further separation and countability results use the corresponding base and exact chart-coordinate hypotheses, not continuous scalar operations or a global fixed model.
  • Stable complements. Over a compact normal base, a finite-rank atlas over real or complex scalars has a defined stable complement in the finite coordinate space of its specified trivial embedding. The complement has its range-graph finite-rank atlas, and its sum with the original family has a full-base trivialization in that coordinate space. The ranks may vary; their pointwise sum is the ambient dimension. The original total-space topology is unchanged. Separately, algebraic split families in a finite-dimensional normed space over a complete nontrivially normed field, with continuous original-total embedding and retraction, have a fixed-model sum chart over an arbitrary topological base, without topologies on the original fibres or their common model.
  • Sharp projection criteria. A fiber-bundle projection is an open embedding exactly when every original fiber is subsingleton, and proper exactly when each fiber is compact. Its total space is second-countable exactly when the base or model is empty, or both are second-countable, without separation hypotheses. Further compactness, separability and Lindelöf criteria are in the detailed guide.
