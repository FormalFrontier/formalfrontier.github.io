Unless stated otherwise, rings are commutative and modules are additive commutative groups. Write C = AdicCompletion I R, H = AdicCompletion I M and P = C ⊗[R] M, with its first-factor C-action. These results use Mathlib's raw inverse-limit completion, not a new construction of completion. The linked catalogue contains the full statements, further result families, examples and boundary cases.

  • Finite cotangents of ideal powers. For any ideal I, finite generation of I / I² over R / I implies finite generation of Iⁿ / I²ⁿ over R / Iⁿ for every n ≥ 0, with the canonical quotient-ring action. No finite generation of I or Noetherianity of R is required. See cotangent modules.
  • Finite modules over complete APf bases. Suppose I has finite-submodule adic cofinality: the intrinsic filtration on each finitely generated submodule of each finite module is cofinal with the induced filtration. If original R is intrinsically I-adically complete, every finite original R-module M is intrinsically complete, and its canonical M → AdicCompletion I M is bijective. More generally, such finite M is I-Hausdorff whenever I ≤ jac(ann_R M). No finite generation of I, original finite presentation, Noetherianity, flatness or prior module separation is required; ring and module universes are independent. See the complete-base criteria.
  • Intrinsic completeness of arbitrary submodules over complete AP bases. Full submodule adic cofinality asks for the same intrinsic/induced filtration comparison for every submodule of each finite module, without finite generation of the submodule. Over an intrinsically I-adically complete original ring R, every original R-submodule N of a finite R-module is intrinsically I-adically complete. No finite generation of N or I, Noetherianity, finite presentation, flatness or additional Jacobson-radical assumption is required. The conclusion concerns the intrinsic ideal-power topology on N, separately from ambient closedness. See the complete-base criteria.
  • Adically closed submodules with finite quotient. If I has finite-submodule adic cofinality and M / N is finite over original R, then I ≤ jac(ann_R(M / N)) makes N closed in the canonical I-adic module topology of M. Neither ambient M nor N needs to be finite. No finite generation of I, Noetherianity, completeness or ambient separation is assumed; ring and module universes are independent. This is a closedness result, not an intrinsic-completeness assertion for N. See module-adic topologies.
  • Noetherian raw completion. For any ideal I, C is Noetherian iff R / I is Noetherian and I / I² is finitely generated over R / I; equivalently, iff R / I^n is Noetherian for any chosen n ≥ 2. Neither I.FG nor Noetherianity of R is required. See cotangent criteria.
  • Completion of a Noetherian original module. For any ideal I, a Noetherian original R-module M has raw completion H Noetherian over C with its canonical action. No Noetherianity of R or R / I, ideal finiteness, flatness, faithfulness, separation or completeness is assumed; the converse fails. See Noetherian modules.
  • Faithful completed scalar action. For any ideal I, if original M is Noetherian over R and the original R-action is faithful, the canonical C-action on H is faithful by an automatic FaithfulSMul instance. Ring and module universes are independent; no ideal finiteness, separation or completeness is assumed. See faithful actions.
  • Annihilator of a completed Noetherian original module. For any ideal I and Noetherian original R-module M, put K = ann_R M and let q : R → R/K be the quotient map. Then ann_C H is the kernel of the canonical map C → AdicCompletion (I.map q) (R/K). No Noetherianity of R or C, ideal finiteness, flatness, faithfulness, separation or completeness is assumed. The effective coefficient ring C / ann_C H is canonically isomorphic as an original-R algebra to AdicCompletion (I.map q) (R/K). On every completed coefficient, the equivalence and its inverse commute with the quotient map. The annihilator need not be the ideal generated by K in C. See annihilators.
  • Jacobson criteria under finite-submodule adic cofinality. If I.HasFiniteSubmoduleAdicCofinality, then I ≤ jac(0) iff every finite original R-module is intrinsically I-Hausdorff; equivalently every submodule of every such module is closed in its canonical ambient power topology. The universal conditions quantify over modules in the ring's universe; finite-module representatives transport uses to independent universes with the original action. No ideal finiteness, Noetherianity or nontriviality is assumed. See separation criteria.
  • Pointwise-cofinal submodule inclusions. For a submodule K of an original R-module N, suppose that for each n some m satisfies K ⊓ I^m N ≤ I^n K, with both sides viewed inside N. Then completion of K.subtype is injective and C-linear. The source uses the intrinsic I-adic filtration on K. No finiteness or Noetherianity of the ring, ideal or modules, flatness, separation or completeness is assumed; ring and module universes are independent. See the cofinality criterion.
  • Intrinsic precompleteness of closed cofinal submodules. If the original R-module M is I-precomplete, M / N is I-Hausdorff, and the induced filtration on N is pointwise cofinal with its intrinsic one, then N is intrinsically I-precomplete. Ambient separation or completeness is not needed, and there is no finiteness or Noetherianity assumption on the ring, ideal or modules. The scalar action remains the original R-action. For a complete ambient module, existing separation inheritance gives completeness of N. See submodules.
  • Injections into Noetherian original targets. For every ideal I, an injective original linear map f : M →ₗ[R] N induces an injective canonical map on raw completions if N is Noetherian over original R. The ring, source and target universes are independent. No Noetherianity of R, finite generation of I, source finiteness, flatness, separation or completeness is assumed. Module Artin–Rees supplies the pointwise-cofinality bound for the original range, giving this application of the subtype criterion. See completion injectivity.
  • Completion of original images. For every ideal I and original linear map f : M →ₗ[R] N, completion of the original range inclusion has the same C-submodule range as the completed map, without Noetherian hypotheses. If K = f.range satisfies the pointwise-cofinality bound above, AdicCompletion I (LinearMap.range f) ≃ₗ[C] LinearMap.range (AdicCompletion.map I f) via that completed inclusion. The original range uses its intrinsic I-adic filtration; M is arbitrary and f need not be injective or surjective. No finiteness or Noetherianity of the ring, ideal or modules, flatness, separation or completeness is assumed; all three universes are independent. For Noetherian original N, the original Noetherian-target equivalence agrees with this comparison for every proof of the bound. See the range comparison and Noetherian-target application.
  • Middle exactness with a Noetherian original middle module. For any ideal I, an original exact pair M →ₗ[R] N →ₗ[R] P remains middle exact under the canonical completed maps if N is Noetherian over original R and the original last map is surjective. The first source is arbitrary and the first map need not be injective. No ring Noetherianity, ideal finiteness, flatness, faithfulness, separation or completeness is assumed; all four universes are independent. See completion exactness.
  • Middle exactness from two pointwise range bounds. For any ideal I, an original exact pair M →ₗ[R] N →ₗ[R] P remains middle exact under the canonical completed maps if, separately for each original range K in its ambient module X, every n admits some m with K ⊓ I^m X ≤ I^n K. The two bounds may have different witnesses. Neither map needs to be injective or surjective. No finiteness or Noetherianity of the ring, ideal or modules, flatness, separation or completeness is assumed; all four universes are independent. See completion exactness.
  • Middle exactness for Noetherian original pairs. For any ideal I, an original exact pair M →ₗ[R] N →ₗ[R] P remains middle exact under the canonical completed maps if both original N and P are Noetherian over original R. The last map need not be surjective, the first map need not be injective, and M is arbitrary. No ring Noetherianity, ideal finiteness, flatness, faithfulness, separation or completeness is assumed; all four universes are independent. See completion exactness.
  • Completion of original kernels. For every ideal I and original linear map g : N →ₗ[R] P with both original modules Noetherian over original R, AdicCompletion I (LinearMap.ker g) ≃ₗ[C] LinearMap.ker (AdicCompletion.map I g). The forward map is completion of the original kernel inclusion, using the intrinsic filtration on that kernel. No surjectivity or injectivity of g, ring Noetherianity, ideal finiteness, flatness, faithfulness, separation or completeness is assumed; ring and module universes are independent. The inverse gives unique factorization of maps into the completed-map kernel and agrees with the split comparison on overlap. See kernel comparison.
  • Noetherian original module quotients. For any ideal and n ≥ 2, the original M / IⁿM is Noetherian over R iff M / IM is Noetherian over R and the image of IM in M / I²M is finitely generated over original R. Neither R nor R / I need be Noetherian. See the original-quotient criterion.
  • Noetherian completed modules. For any ideal and original module, H is Noetherian over C iff the original M / I²M is Noetherian over R; equivalently, iff any chosen M / IⁿM, n ≥ 2, is Noetherian over R / Iⁿ with its canonical action. No Noetherian coefficient ring, finite generation, original completeness or separation is required. Noetherianity of the original associated graded module over the quotient Rees algebra also suffices. See the module criterion.
  • Module completion and stationary evaluation. If the image of I M in the original M / I² M is finitely generated over R, then Iⁿ H = ker(evalₙ) for every n ≥ 0 and H is intrinsically I-adically complete for its original R-action, without assuming I.FG. These kernel and completeness conclusions also hold if H is Noetherian over C, without Noetherianity of C; see the module criterion. More generally, if Iⁿ H = ker(evalₙ) for all n, restriction along of I M gives an R-linear Hom equivalence from maps H →ₗ[R] N to maps M →ₗ[R] N for every intrinsically I-adically complete target N, with inverse canonical extension; N needs no C-action. Both source hypotheses above, or finite generation of a positive power of I, supply these kernel identities. See linear maps. For any ideal and n ≥ 0, evaluation at n on H is injective iff I^(n+1) M = I^n M. Under this equality, H ≃ₗ[R] M / I^n M; equality of ideal powers gives the corresponding R-algebra equivalence for C. See kernels, completeness and stationary evaluation.
  • Finite presentation and flatness. If H is flat over C, then H is finitely presented iff any chosen positive original quotient M / IⁿM is finitely presented over R / Iⁿ; when this holds, H is finite projective and every original quotient is finitely presented. If R / I is Noetherian, flat H is finitely presented iff it is finite over C, and is then projective, without Noetherianity of C. Separately, if C is Noetherian, H is flat iff every positive original quotient is flat over R / Iⁿ; in particular, a flat original M has flat raw completion. See finite presentation and raw flatness.
  • Tensor, exactness and Hom comparisons. If the original M / IM is finitely generated over R, then H is finite over C and P → H is onto. Projectivity of P over C, not merely of H, gives injectivity for every ideal; together these premises give an equivalence. Short exact sequences with flat original last module remain short exact after completion. The completed Hom identifies with the C-linear Hom between completions when the original source is finite projective, or is projective and I.FG; the target is arbitrary. See tensor, exactness and Hom. If every original-R power IⁿH is ker(evalₙ), every R-linear H → AdicCompletion I N respects the canonical C-actions, for arbitrary N without intrinsic completeness of N or its raw completion. If original M is also finite projective, the map is uniquely represented by a completed original Hom element after restricting the comparison's scalars to R.
  • Finite products of coefficient rings. For a finite family of commutative rings, put A = Π i, R i and let I be any ideal of A. Completion of A is canonically A-algebra equivalent to the product of the completions at the coordinate-image ideals. For an arbitrary family of original R i-modules, the corresponding module comparison is A-linear and intertwines the completed coefficient actions. Both comparisons commute with original representatives and every finite reduction, including precision zero. The index, ring and module universes are independent; empty products and zero rings are included, without finite-generation, Noetherianity, completeness or separatedness assumptions. See coefficient products.
  • Maps and changes of ideal. For every ideal and linear f : M → N, completion of f is onto iff the original M → N / IN is onto. Mutual containment of powers gives an R-linear equivalence of completions, including at I^k and I for positive k. Completion at the product of a finite pairwise-comaximal family is R-linearly equivalent to the product of the completions. Arbitrary dependent products commute with completion if some given positive power of I is finitely generated. See maps, cofinality, CRT and products. A ring map carrying one ideal into another induces a homomorphism between their completions. For an R-algebra, the completed coefficient map gives the named, non-global AdicCompletion.extendedAlgebra; existing coefficient algebras are unchanged. See completed ring maps.
  • Henselian lifting. Over a Henselian pair (R,I), a finite square polynomial system with unit Jacobian determinant at a root modulo I has a unique root in that residue class. Every specified R-algebra map from an étale algebra to R/I lifts uniquely to an R-algebra map to R. See Hensel lifting and étale points; determinant- and resultant-square refinements and Henselian-pair criteria are in the expanded catalogue.
  • Action nilpotence and ideal powers. For a finitely generated ideal of a commutative semiring acting on an additive commutative monoid module, some ideal power annihilates the whole module iff I ≤ radical(ann M); the module need not be finitely generated. Over a Bézout commutative semiring, x ∈ I^n iff some t ∈ I satisfies t^n ∣ x, including n = 0. See torsion and powers.
  • Maximal ideals in the adic topology. For any ideal I of a commutative ring, a maximal ideal m is closed in the I-adic topology iff I ≤ m. Thus every maximal ideal is closed iff I lies in the Jacobson radical. No APf, finiteness, Noetherianity, completeness or separation is assumed; the global criterion includes the zero ring. See ideal topologies.
  • Compactness and valuation completeness. For a given compatible additive uniformity with IsAdic I, a ring is compact iff it is uniformly complete and all R/I^n are finite; under I.FG, finiteness of R/I suffices. For valuation integers, a nonzero generator with separated principal powers identifies adic completeness with completeness for the given valuation uniformity. See compactness and valuation completeness.
  • Teichmüller and Witt structure. For prime p, a surjection from a kernel-adically complete ring to a perfect characteristic-p ring has a unique multiplicative zero-and-one section, whose image is the intersection of the p^n-power ranges. The reduced-residue perfection extension is described in results and models. Witt vectors form a domain iff the coefficient ring is a domain of characteristic p, and a DVR iff it is a perfect field of characteristic p. See Teichmüller maps, domains and DVRs.
