These results are proved here for representations over a ring k equipped with a topology. The group, discreteness and action-continuity hypotheses are specified separately below; foundations come from Mathlib.

Finite-quotient descent, torsion and the topological colimit

See CompactDiscreteTorsion and FiniteStageColimit for these APIs.

For a compact topological group G and discrete X : TopRep k G over a ring with a supplied topology, the homogeneous cochain complex is the colimit of its open-normal finite-quotient cochain complexes, with coefficients in X^U at a stage U. This is a colimit in complexes of topological modules and separately in every degree. Joint continuity of the action is not needed. The quotient-stage diagram and inflation cocone also have coefficient naturality without compactness or discreteness assumptions. The integer sign example uses a nonconstant cochain and checks the descendant equation, uniqueness of its lift at a chosen stage, and compatibility with every refinement.

Separately, for a compact topological group G and discrete X : TopRep k G with [Ring k] [TopologicalSpace k], every continuous cohomology class lifts from an open-normal finite quotient with its quotient-invariant coefficients. In positive degrees a class-dependent quotient order annihilates the class; this gives additive torsion, not a uniform bound. Joint continuity of the action is not needed for these class-lift and torsion statements, nor for the cohomology colimit. The open-normal cohomology inflation cocone is a filtered colimit in topological modules in every degree, including zero, with arbitrary topological-module targets. It uses the cohomology topology, algebraic boundaries and class-dependent eventual refinements, not fixed-stage positive-degree injectivity, a uniform refinement or a closed-boundary-range assumption. The concrete sign-orbit cochain construction still uses joint continuity.

Additivity in coefficient maps in every degree

For [Ring k] [TopologicalSpace k], topological groups H and G, and a continuous homomorphism φ : H →ₜ* G, the coefficient-map API proves additivity and preservation of zero in the coefficient morphism in every natural degree, including zero. For a fixed acting group, ContinuousCohomology.coefficientFunctor n is an additive functor on topological representations, using the existing continuous-cohomology objects and maps. In degree zero, ContinuousCohomology.zeroNatIso naturally identifies this fixed-group functor with invariants; its forward and inverse components are the canonical ContinuousCohomology.zeroIso maps. The same ring with its supplied topology suffices: neither commutativity nor continuity of the ring operations, compactness of the groups nor discreteness of the coefficients is required. The integer model uses the one-element group PUnit acting trivially on ℤ: the sum of two identity coefficient morphisms doubles every class in every degree. Its degree-zero calculation uses zeroNatIso naturality to send the class with invariant value 1 to a nonzero class with invariant value 2.

Positive coinduced cohomology after closed restriction

For compact topological groups and discrete coefficients, diagonal insertion gives a map on the recursively iterated coinduced homogeneous cochains with an explicit evaluation law. This insertion contracts the cochain complex in every positive degree, so continuous cohomology with coinduced discrete coefficients vanishes there. Degree-zero vanishing is false even for the two-element group acting trivially on nonzero integers (see the two-element-group client). The coefficient functor for a fixed acting group and degree uses Mathlib's continuous-cohomology maps; its object and morphism laws transport positive vanishing across coefficient isomorphisms in the same group. For compact Hausdorff totally disconnected G, the closed-subgroup positive-vanishing theorem then applies to every closed H, and also to restrictions of representations isomorphic to TopRep.coind₁ B. The restricted representation is H-coinduced from all of C(G ⧸ H, B) with its generally nontrivial pointwise H-action. Only discrete B is needed; neither openness or normality of H nor joint continuity of the coefficient action is required. The finite signed S₃ clients instantiate positive vanishing at a nonnormal subgroup, not at a closed nonopen subgroup. These are strictly positive-degree conclusions, corresponding to the closed-subgroup clause of Neukirch–Schmidt–Wingberg, I §3, Proposition 1.3.7; they do not assert degree-zero vanishing or all-integer Tate acyclicity.

Normal-quotient coinduction

For [Ring k] [TopologicalSpace k], a compact topological group G, any normal subgroup N and a discrete, jointly continuous B : TopRep k G, the normal-quotient coinduction equivalence identifies TopRep.quotientInvariants N (TopRep.coind₁ B) with TopRep.coind₁ (TopRep.of (ContRepresentation.trivial k (G ⧸ N) B)) as representations of G ⧸ N. This is universe-polymorphic and requires no closedness or openness of N, Hausdorff condition or choice of section. Its forward and inverse maps satisfy (P f)(mk N t) = B.ρ t⁻¹ (f t) and (J F)(t) = B.ρ t (F (mk N t)). The right-hand coefficient is the whole B, not B^N, and has trivial quotient action. The sign-product and full-group clients exhibit B^N = 0 with nonzero B, a quotient function moved by the residual quotient action, and an invariant function whose value at the identity is moved by N.

Degreewise exactness under coefficient embeddings

For any topological group G and A B C : TopRep.{max v w} k G, cochainsMap_exact_of_isEmbedding proves that an exact underlying coefficient row A → B → C whose first map is a topological embedding induces an exact row of homogeneous invariant cochains in every natural degree. No local compactness, coefficient discreteness or extra joint-action continuity is required. The original cochainsMap_exact is the discrete-middle, injective-first-map corollary. This is underlying algebraic exactness, not topological strictness or an exactness assertion on cohomology. The separate cochain-surjectivity theorem still assumes locally compact G, a discrete target and jointly continuous source coefficients.

Algebraic connecting maps, naturality and initial invariants

The algebraic connecting-map guide describes a connecting k-linear map in each natural degree and proves the three recurring exactness assertions for the underlying modules of continuous cohomology. They use locally compact G, discrete B and C, jointly continuous action on B, injective i, exact A → B → C, and surjective p. The connecting construction uses the cochain short exact sequence and Mathlib's homology boundary; its representative formula and comparison with the topologically split degree-zero map specify its sign. The algebraic assertions also show that vanishing of the underlying Hⁿ(G,B) makes the connector mono, vanishing of Hⁿ⁺¹(G,B) makes it epi, and both together give an isomorphism whose forward arrow is the existing connector, for every natural n, including zero. They do not establish continuity of this new map. For the integer sign action on ℤ --×2→ ℤ → ZMod 2, the quotient invariant 1 has a nonzero connecting class; its lack of an invariant integer lift is independently verified. The coinduced two-point row embeds trivial integers as constant functions and projects to signed integers by subtracting the values at the two units. Its middle cohomology vanishes in degrees one and two; the existing exactness statements show that its degree-one connector has nonzero image and is surjective, without using a general connecting-isomorphism result in these independent witness proofs. Separate private clients apply the general mono, epi and isomorphism APIs to this row. Two split rows give complementary degree-zero boundaries: for Multiplicative (ZMod 2), coinduced B has H¹(G,B) = 0 but its degree-zero connector is not mono; for the distinct group ℤˣ, the middle signed coefficient B has H⁰(G,B) = 0 but its degree-zero connector is not epi. In that second row, the final coefficient C is the zero representation on PUnit.

Under the same short-exact-row and topological hypotheses, the initial-invariants module proves exactness of the underlying-module segment 0 → Aᴳ → Bᴳ → Cᴳ → H¹(G,A) → H¹(G,B) → H¹(G,C) at its five internal positions. It uses Mathlib's invariant functor and natural degree-zero isomorphism and the existing algebraic connector. The coefficient-level lemmas TopRep.mapInvariants_injective and TopRep.exact_mapInvariants have weaker hypotheses. Their earlier ContinuousCohomology names remain deprecated aliases. The example checks a nonzero displayed invariant inclusion and a nonsplit sign-action boundary. Its coefficient-level obstruction and existing nonzero connector are independent of the segment theorem. Neither surjectivity at H¹(G,C) nor continuity of the connector is claimed.

For a ring with a supplied topology and any monoid, TopRep.mono_iff_injective characterizes monomorphisms by injectivity, with independent ring, monoid and carrier universes. The action operators are individually continuous; no jointly continuous action or topology on the monoid is assumed. For topological groups, ContinuousCohomology.map_zero_mono preserves monicity under any continuous group homomorphism, and the existing coefficientFunctor 0 preserves monomorphisms. No group-map surjectivity or all-degree preservation is asserted. The integer-doubling example uses a nonsurjective map of the two-element group and nonzero degree-zero classes.

The coefficient-naturality theorem proves that, for two short exact coefficient sequences over the same locally compact G, with the preceding discreteness and joint-continuity hypotheses in each row, commuting coefficient maps (α, β, γ) give δ ≫ Hⁿ⁺¹(α) = Hⁿ(γ) ≫ δ′ for every natural degree. This is an equality of underlying-module maps, not continuity of δ, change-of-group naturality, or a topological delta-functor. The nonidentity sign-sequence client independently verifies the coefficient squares and a nonzero boundary, then applies the naturality theorem.
