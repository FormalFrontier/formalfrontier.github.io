• Integral domains over henselian local rings are local. Let R and S be commutative rings in independent universes, with a given R-algebra structure on S. If R is henselian local, S is a nonzero domain and S is integral over R, then HenselianLocalRing.isLocalRing_of_isIntegral R S gives IsLocalRing S. The base R need not be a domain or reduced; injectivity of the algebra map, module finiteness, Noetherianity and completeness are not assumed. Import IntegralClosure.Henselian. The unital-homomorphism form RingHom.isLocalRing_of_henselian_of_isIntegral alpha h instead takes alpha : R →+* S with h : alpha.IsIntegral, without a chosen algebra structure.
  • For a homomorphism f : A →+* B of commutative rings, if each target element has a positive power in the image, contraction is an order isomorphism from radical ideals of B to radical ideals of A containing RingHom.ker f. Its inverse is extension followed by radical. For any integral homomorphism of commutative rings and any source ideal I, Ideal.comap f (Ideal.map f I).radical = (I ⊔ RingHom.ker f).radical. See the ideal extension and contraction API.

For a homomorphism f : A →+* B from a possibly noncommutative semiring A to a commutative semiring B, the ideal API shows that positive powers of each target generator in the image of f suffice to identify the radicals of the generated ideal and the extension of its contraction. The exponent may vary with the generator. Under the global power-image hypothesis, contraction is injective on radical ideals of B; the existing prime-spectrum injectivity criterion follows because prime ideals are radical.

  • Integrality reflects along nil kernels. Let K be a commutative ring, let A and B be possibly noncommutative rings with their given K-algebra structures, and let f : A →ₐ[K] B. If every element killed by f is nilpotent, then integrality over K of f a implies integrality of a. The rings may lie in three independent universes; neither injectivity nor surjectivity of f, nor a uniform nilpotence exponent for its kernel, is required. The elementwise theorem IsIntegral.of_isNilpotent_of_map_eq_zero and its whole-algebra counterpart Algebra.IsIntegral.of_isNilpotent_of_map_eq_zero are in IntegralClosure.RingHom. The latter reflects integrality of B/K to A/K; it does not require module finiteness.
  • Finiteness from powers of algebra generators. Let R and A be commutative semirings with the given R-algebra structure. If a finite set generates A as an R-algebra and C : Subalgebra R A contains a positive power of each chosen generator, then the original A is finite as a module over C through the canonical inclusion action. Exponents may vary with the generator; no field, Noetherianity, finite type of C/R, or ring structure is assumed. The theorem Subalgebra.finite_of_adjoin_eq_top_of_forall_exists_pow_mem bounds powers in each singleton adjoin and multiplies the resulting C-submodule spans. Its finite-type, all-elements corollary is Subalgebra.finite_of_forall_exists_pow_mem. The generator-only criterion applies to ℕ[X²] ⊊ ℕ[X], with generator X and exponent 2, where 1,X span over ℕ[X²]. The all-elements condition fails in this example: the X-coefficient of (1+X)^n is n for every positive n. In general the bounded monomials span; they need not be a basis.
  • Prime-spectrum embeddings from powers in the image. For commutative semirings A and B in independent universes and a homomorphism f : A →+* B, if every b : B has a positive natural power in Set.range f, then PrimeSpectrum.comap f is a topological embedding. The exponent may depend on b; neither injectivity nor surjectivity of f is assumed. The theorem PrimeSpectrum.isEmbedding_comap_of_forall_pow_mem_range combines the inducing criterion PrimeSpectrum.comap_isInducing_of_forall_pow_mem_range with the existing PrimeSpectrum.comap_injective_of_forall_pow_mem_range. Mathlib's basic-open basis and power identities give the induced topology. This separates and generalizes the ring-free embedding component of PrimeSpectrum.isHomeomorph_comap, formalized in Mathlib by Christian Merten and Junyan Xu following the Stacks Project, Tag 0BR8. Mathlib's stronger homeomorphism theorem additionally assumes commutative rings and a kernel consisting of nilpotents. The surjective embedding criterion is recovered with exponent one, but not every localization embedding satisfies the premise. Over arbitrary commutative semirings this asserts no spectral surjectivity, homeomorphism, universal injectivity or closed range.
  • Integral closedness descends along a linear retraction. For commutative rings A and B, an injective algebra map A → B into an integrally closed domain, together with an A-linear left inverse B →ₗ[A] A, gives integral closedness of A. The theorem IsIntegrallyClosed.of_linearRetract derives the domain structure on A inside its proof. The retraction need not preserve multiplication, and the algebra map need not be integral.
  • Normal-domain descent from any field scalar extension. For any field extension l/k and commutative k-algebra A, if A ⊗[k] l is a domain, then so is A; if the tensor product is also integrally closed, then so is A. The separate theorems isDomain_left and isIntegrallyClosed_left in Algebra.TensorProduct require no finite-dimensionality, algebraicity or separability assumption on the field extension.
  • Tensor-factor evaluation gives linear retractions. For the same fields and algebra, any k-linear functional f : l →ₗ[k] k defines an A-linear rightLinearMap from A ⊗[k] l to A, taking a ⊗ₜ[k] b to a * algebraMap k A (f b). When f 1 = 1, rightLinearMap_includeLeft proves it is a left inverse to includeLeft. Normalization is needed for this retraction equation, not for constructing the evaluation map; the functional and evaluation map need not be multiplicative.
  • Monic relations at inverses of units. For a division semiring R, a semiring S, an arbitrary unital homomorphism f : R →+* S and a unit a : Sˣ, any nonzero polynomial relation over R at a yields a monic polynomial relation at a⁻¹. The results in IntegralClosure.Polynomial require no coefficient centrality or commutativity of S. The more general semiring-source theorem assumes that the reversed polynomial's leading coefficient is a unit in R, without requiring the original polynomial to be nonzero.
  • A unit-constant criterion for two monic relations. For arbitrary semirings R and S, a unital homomorphism f : R →+* S and a : Sˣ, monic polynomial relations at both a and a⁻¹ exist exactly when a monic relation at a has constant coefficient a unit in R. When both relations exist, that constant coefficient can be chosen to be 1. These results in IntegralClosure.Polynomial use left coefficients and require no coefficient centrality. The unit condition is in the source semiring, not merely after applying f.
  • Units descend from a monic inverse relation. For arbitrary semirings R and S, an injective homomorphism f : R →+* S, r : R and a unit a : Sˣ with ↑a = f r, a monic polynomial relation over R at a⁻¹ implies IsUnit r. The theorem RingHom.isUnit_of_monic_eval₂_units_inv_eq_zero in IntegralClosure.RingHom needs neither commutativity nor a second relation at a. Although stated with semiring instances, its hypotheses force both semirings to admit ring structures.

The five descent and tensor-evaluation declarations build on mathlib's integral-closure, fraction-ring, tensor-product and projective-module duality infrastructure. They establish descent, not ascent, a converse or normalization. The descent theorems return typeclass proofs for local use rather than installing global instances; the tensor evaluation construction is noncomputable.

The reciprocal-evaluation lemmas generalize mathlib's commutative-target identities using its polynomial-reversal and finite-sum APIs. Unit descent refines its RingHom.IsIntegral.isLocalHom argument and uses Group Theory's zero-sum unit-transfer API. Subalgebra finiteness uses mathlib's adjoin-span and adjoin-union formulas and finite generation of submodule products.

The henselian locality result generalizes the valuation-ring setting of I. B. Fesenko and S. V. Vostokov, Local Fields and Their Extensions, second edition (2002), to henselian local base rings that need not be domains. Its one-element annihilator argument uses lifted coprime factors and a domain contradiction. It reuses HenselianRing.exists_monic_factorization from Formal Frontier's Adic Modules library.

Relative integrality and positive-power criteria

Let R be a commutative ring, S a possibly noncommutative ring, and f : R →+* S a ring homomorphism; the rings may lie in independent universes. An element x : S is integral relative to f (f.IsIntegralElem x) if some monic polynomial p : R[X] satisfies p.eval₂ f x = 0.

  • Integrality is unchanged by positive powers. RingHom.IsIntegralElem.pow shows that every natural power of an integral element is integral, including exponent zero. Conversely, of_pow and the simp lemma pow_iff require 0 < n: integrality of x ^ n then implies, and is equivalent to, integrality of x.
  • Positive powers in the range imply an integral homomorphism. RingHom.isIntegral_of_forall_pow_mem_range proves f.IsIntegral if each x : S has some positive power in Set.range f. Each element may have its own exponent; neither a uniform exponent nor surjectivity is required.

These criteria require no Algebra structure, centrality of coefficient images, domain, nontriviality or finiteness hypothesis. Import IntegralClosure.RingHom or the root IntegralClosure; the manual reference explains the arguments and the supporting polynomial evaluation lemmas.

Jacobson radicals under integral homomorphisms

For arbitrary commutative rings R and S in independent universes and an integral ring homomorphism f : R →+* S, IntegralClosure.Jacobson provides:

  • Ideal.comap_jacobson_of_isIntegral: for every target ideal J : Ideal S, J.jacobson.comap f = (J.comap f).jacobson.
  • Ideal.map_jacobson_le_of_isIntegral: for every source ideal I : Ideal R, I.jacobson.map f ≤ (I.map f).jacobson.
  • Ideal.map_le_jacobson_bot_of_isIntegral: for every source ideal I, I ≤ (⊥ : Ideal R).jacobson implies I.map f ≤ (⊥ : Ideal S).jacobson.

No injectivity, surjectivity or nontriviality assumption is required. Mathlib's surjective Jacobson formula also applies to possibly noncommutative rings; the integral formula here assumes commutativity.
