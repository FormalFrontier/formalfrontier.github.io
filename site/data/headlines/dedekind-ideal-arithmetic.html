• Norm detects strict ideal inclusion and equality. In an infinite commutative Dedekind domain with finite quotients, I < J and I ≠ ⊥ imply J.absNorm < I.absNorm. If I ≤ J and the norms agree, I = J, including zero ideals. No finite-free presentation over ℤ is needed. See strict comparison and equality.
  • A supplied prime-norm element yields a principal prime. In an infinite Dedekind domain finite free over ℤ, a natural prime p and a supplied α with (Algebra.norm ℤ α).natAbs = p give a principal prime above (p). This is conditional existence, not a search for α, a class-number computation or a witness assertion for every prime. See principal-prime extraction.
  • Localization can kill the class group and give a PID/UFD. For Dedekind domains A, B in possibly different universes, an A-algebra B torsion-free over A, and IsLocalization M B, extension Cl(A) → Cl(B) is surjective. If every source class is an integral power of a supplied g and g extends to one, the library gives a PID and hence a UFD. Surjectivity needs no generator; neither class group need be finite.
  • Section indices and common multiples. Over a domain, scaling a nonzero section of a torsion-free module multiplies its quotient index by the principal left-ideal index, including zero scalars and infinite quotients. Invertible modules over commutative domains with finite quotients have finite quotients by nonzero sections; over any commutative domain their nonzero sections admit common nonzero scalar multiples. See module indices.
  • Intrinsic heights of normed invertible modules. Over 𝓞 K for a number field K, definite seminorms on all archimedean fibres give a positive, section-independent height: quotient index divided by the weighted norm product. Invertibility, not integral freeness, is assumed. Tensor products, positive rescaling and integral-equivalence pullback obey the expected multiplicative and logarithmic laws. The normalized logarithmic height can be negative. See module heights and the height guide.
  • Rational-frame and finite-place height formulas. Every rational frame evaluates this same intrinsic height as 1 / (N(I) * archimedeanProduct). Nonzero fractional-ideal norms factor into prime norms with signed exponents over Dedekind domains finite free over ℤ, without a number-field structure on the fraction field. For number fields the height numerator uses residue cardinalities with negative coefficient-ideal counts. See fractional norms and finite-place height factors.
  • Principal exponents determine scalar completion norms. Over a Dedekind domain and fraction field, a nonzero scalar has adic valuation equal to the exponential of its negative principal-ideal count. For an infinite base with finite quotients, its completion norm is the residue cardinality to that negative count. No norm on bare K is chosen. See adic norms.
  • Sharp signed fractional-ideal exponents. At each fixed height-one prime of any Dedekind domain, a nonzero fractional ideal contains a nonzero element attaining its signed count. Together with Mathlib's count_mono, this is an attained minimum. The witness may depend on the prime; no principality, infinite-ring, finite-residue or ℤ-freeness hypothesis is needed. See count attainment.
  • Algebraic integral spans in adic fibres. For any R-module M over a Dedekind domain with fraction field K and height-one prime p, the span of 1 ⊗ m over the valuation integers in F ⊗[R] M, F = p.adicCompletion K, is the image of O ⊗[R] M, O = p.adicCompletionIntegers K. It is finite over O if M is finite, and spans the whole fibre over F; flatness makes the image map an equivalence onto the span. For M = R, the whole span corresponds exactly to O. No finite-residue or infinite-ring hypothesis is used. See integral spans.
  • Canonical ultrametric seminorms on adic fibres. For an infinite Dedekind domain R with finite quotients, fraction field K, height-one prime p and invertible M, the fibre F ⊗[R] M has a canonical definite F-seminorm. In every rational frame, on every vector, it is the coordinate norm times the residue cardinality raised to the signed coefficient-ideal count, with a plus sign; negative counts are included. It is frame-independent and characterized by its rational-unit-vector weight. The supplied-frame constructor only needs finite M and a frame, allowing torsion. For M = R the whole seminorm is the completion norm pulled back along tensor right-unit equivalence. See adic seminorms.
