• Ranges of finitely generated algebras: over any commutative ring, taking the range of an algebra map with finitely generated domain is left adjoint to the inclusion of finitely generated subalgebra stages. The stage functor is therefore final. The ambient algebra need not be finitely generated, the base and carrier universes are independent, and neither the given map nor the scalar map need be injective.
  • Factorization on finite-type points: over any commutative ring K, a morphism f : X ⟶ Y with X locally of finite type over Spec K factors through a monomorphism i : N ⟶ Y if and only if every point of X over a finitely generated K-algebra lifts through i after composition with f. The factorization is in schemes over the same Spec K. No finiteness, affineness or separatedness condition is imposed on N or Y; the source's local finite-type hypothesis is needed.
  • Points over finitely generated subalgebras: for any given tower of commutative rings K → R → S, every S-point of a scheme locally of finite type over Spec K factors through a finitely generated R-subalgebra A ⊆ S. The forward map is the algebra-spectrum map of the inclusion, and the composite equals the original point over the original Spec K. Two stage points agreeing over S agree over a common finitely generated subalgebra. No affineness, quasi-compactness or separatedness of the tested scheme, injectivity of either tower map, or finite generation of R over K is required. These statements concern subalgebra inclusions, not arbitrary filtered algebra colimits.
  • Morphisms from dense affine-point subfunctors: for arbitrary schemes X and Y over any base scheme B, let N_B be the full affine-point functor over B. If D ⊆ N_B(X) is one-cover-dense for flat, surjective morphisms of affine B-schemes, every natural transformation α : D ⟶ N_B(Y) is induced by a unique morphism f : X ⟶ Y over the same base, with D.ι ≫ N_B.map f = α (Scheme.existsUnique_hom_of_isOneCoverDense). No sheaf condition on X or D, finite-product condition on D, or finiteness or separatedness hypothesis is required.
  • Faithful flatness of finite families: a finite family of flat commutative-ring maps that is jointly surjective on prime spectra induces a faithfully flat map to the product. The spectrum implication also holds for arbitrary families of commutative semirings, without flatness. Empty index types and zero rings are included.
  • Flatness under source precomposition: for arbitrary scheme morphisms p : X ⟶ Y and f : Y ⟶ Z, flatness and point-surjectivity of p, together with flatness of p ≫ f, imply flatness of f, without quasi-compactness or finiteness assumptions. The ring-tower theorem cancels a faithfully flat second map from a flat composite of commutative ring maps in independent universes; the composite need not be faithfully flat.
  • Closed points of scheme pullbacks: for specified locally finite-type arrows to the spectrum of any field, the original projections of any specified pullback give a surjection onto pairs of closed points. Over a separably closed field this is a canonical set equivalence, with pairing, projection, uniqueness, eta, diagonal and fixed-base naturality laws. No global finite-type, quasi-compactness, separation or reducedness assumption is imposed. This does not identify the Zariski closed-point topology with the ordinary product topology.
  • Locally closed points in continuous preimages: for arbitrary topological spaces and a continuous map, every nonempty preimage of a locally constructible set has a member at which that same preimage is locally closed. No separation, compactness or Noetherian hypothesis is needed; the result does not assert preservation of constructibility by continuous maps. A dense-preimage application shows that a dense such preimage in a nonempty source has nonempty interior.
  • Closedness through closed points: in any Jacobson topological space, a locally closed subset is closed if and only if its trace in the closed-point subspace is closed. Local closedness is required for the full subset, not just its trace; no separation or finiteness hypothesis is imposed.
  • Cofinite closed-point topology: in any preirreducible Noetherian topological space of Krull dimension at most one, a subset of the closed-point subspace is closed exactly when it is the whole space or finite. Its given topology is therefore cofinite, without separation, sobriety or Jacobson hypotheses. The Zariski closed-point topology of Spec K[X] and the Zariski topology of MaximalSpectrum K[X] are cofinite over every field K.
  • Locally constructible closed-point traces: in any Jacobson topological space, a subset satisfying Mathlib's Topology.IsLocallyConstructible has a dense closed-point trace, and is closed exactly when that trace is closed in the original closed-point subspace. Closed-point traces also detect inclusion and equality between such subsets, with no separation or Noetherian hypothesis. The proofs use density after intersection with arbitrary locally closed subsets, without assuming those subsets are locally constructible. A common density theorem for arbitrary unions of locally closed sets recovers Mathlib's locally closed density theorem by a singleton-index specialization; this union theorem does not assert trace conservativity.
  • Maximal-point locally ringed spaces: for any commutative Jacobson ring R, construct a locally ringed space on the closed-point subspace of Spec R. Its inclusion induces an isomorphism of full sheaves after pushforward, principal-open rings are R_f, stalks are R_m, and global sections recover R, with nilpotents and supplied scalars. Localization-basis recognition identifies any full sheaf with compatible coordinate actions and the same localization property. Morphisms are contravariant for homomorphisms whose contractions preserve maximal ideals; this preservation is derived for finite-type homomorphisms from a Jacobson ring. Full component uniqueness identifies a supplied sheaf component from its global coordinates, without assuming that component is already local. The inclusion need not be an isomorphism of spaces, and no general Scheme structure is asserted for the maximal-point space.
  • Lifts to closed-point targets: for a Jacobson locally ringed target Y and an arbitrary locally ringed source X, closedPointInclusionHomEquiv identifies maps X ⟶ C(Y) with original morphisms X ⟶ Y whose every image point is closed. The inverse is the unique full locally ringed-space lift through the given inclusion. Its section, stalk and global-section maps read back the original maps through the given comparisons. It is natural in every source morphism and in closed-point-preserving morphisms between Jacobson targets, and retains supplied structure-map triangles. Its affine specialization identifies maps X ⟶ C(R) with original maps X ⟶ Spec R whose image ideals are all maximal, for any commutative Jacobson ring R, and reads back the original homomorphism R → Γ(X). No Jacobson hypothesis is imposed on X or its global-section ring.
  • Hom recovery for maximal-point spaces: for commutative Jacobson rings R and S, maximalPointHomEquiv identifies ring homomorphisms R →+* S preserving maximal ideals under contraction with all locally ringed space morphisms C(S) ⟶ C(R), where C(R) = Spec.maximalPointSpace R. The inverse is χS⁻¹ ∘ Γ(g) ∘ χR for the original coordinate isomorphisms, so the full morphism is recovered from its global-section map. This equivalence restricts to supplied scalar maps. For given K-algebra actions in a common universe, it identifies maximal-contraction-preserving algebra homomorphisms with morphisms over Spec K; if S is finite type over K, every algebra homomorphism corresponds, with no finite-type assumption on R.
  • Locality of full ringed morphisms: for commutative Jacobson rings R,S, a ringed morphism g : C(S) ⟶ C(R) is local if and only if its original global-coordinate homomorphism preserves maximal ideals under contraction. Under this condition the entire original morphism is the forgotten maximalPointMap, and its unique locally ringed lift retains all supplied components and respects composition. For given K-algebra structures over any commutative ring K, scalar compatibility and finite type of S over K imply locality, with no finite-type hypothesis on R. The full sheaves retain nilpotents and the zero ring is included.
  • The integers are Jacobson: the canonical integer ring is a Jacobson ring. A polynomial evaluation example identifies the maximal-point morphism over Spec ℤ induced by evaluation at zero, ℤ[X] →ₐ[ℤ] ℤ, together with its recovered ring map and the value X ↦ 0.
  • Reduced projective spectra: for any ℕ-graded commutative ring A with grading 𝒜, Proj 𝒜 is reduced whenever A is reduced, with no nonemptiness or finiteness assumption.
  • Polynomial projective coordinate separation: over any nontrivial commutative ring and any nontrivial variable type, the total-degree polynomial projective spectrum has at least two underlying points. Coordinate points over the same coefficient prime are distinct for distinct indices; each lies in exactly its own coordinate basic open. This includes the projective line over the two-element field, without a domain or field-size bound.
  • Local normality of polynomial rings: over any commutative locally normal base ring, multivariate polynomial rings with arbitrary variable types are locally normal.
  • Global sections under flat scalar extension: for a quasicompact quasiseparated scheme over a commutative ring k and a flat commutative k-algebra K, the canonical equivalence identifies the global sections of the base change with K ⊗[k] Γ(X, ⊤). Its scalar, section and pure tensor formulas retain the two pullback projections. Identity and tower coherence are also available for field extensions.
  • Global sections and affine targets: if p : X ⟶ S induces an isomorphism on global sections and T is affine, precomposition is a bijection (S ⟶ T) → (X ⟶ T), for arbitrary X and S. A reduced connected scheme universally closed over a field has a field of global sections. For a reduced preconnected scheme universally closed over a field, a rational section identifies its global sections with the base field, so every morphism to an affine scheme is constant at that rational point. Direct import for the field criterion and the canonical global-sections isomorphism: SchemeProperties.GlobalSections.Field.
  • Proper affine point criterion: for any field K : Type u and X : Scheme.{u}, a morphism f : X ⟶ Spec (CommRingCat.of K) with [IsProper f] [IsAffine X] [PreconnectedSpace X] gives Subsingleton X (AlgebraicGeometry.Scheme.Hom.subsingleton_of_isProper_of_isAffine). The underlying point set has at most one point; X may be empty or nonreduced. This does not assert a scheme isomorphism or uniqueness of scheme morphisms. Direct import: SchemeProperties.ProperAffineField.
  • Finite-variable polynomial Proj is proper over the original Spec R for every commutative ring R : Type (max u v) and finite σ : Type v, including zero rings and empty σ; see the guide.
  • Rigidity from a collapsed fibre: for a field K, a proper integral K-scheme X whose structure map has a K-rational section, a preconnected K-scheme Y with an arbitrary chosen point y₀, and a K-scheme Z separated over K, a compatible K-morphism h : X ×[K] Y ⟶ Z factors uniquely as h = snd ≫ g for a K-morphism g : Y ⟶ Z if the topological image under h of the projection fibre over y₀ is subsingleton. The factorization is equality of scheme morphisms. The parameter need not be reduced, and y₀ need not be rational or closed. Direct import: SchemeProperties.Rigidity.
  • Real–complex residue degrees: the coproduct Spec ℝ ⨿ Spec ℂ, over Spec ℝ by the identity and the standard real-algebra map to ℂ, has two distinct closed points. Their original residue real algebras are canonically equivalent to ℝ and ℂ and have real dimensions one and two, respectively.
