• Integral closedness of polynomial power subalgebras. For any commutative ring R, the subalgebra generated by X ^ n for n ∈ AddSubmonoid.multiples d is integrally closed iff R[X] is when 0 < d, and iff R is when d = 0. In particular every degree works over an integrally closed domain, by the existing Polynomial.isIntegrallyClosed_adjoin_X_pow_multiples in MonomialIntegralClosure.lean.   • Finite natural-exponent monomial extensions. For an additive submonoid S of ℕ and any subset U, a finite E ⊆ U with U ⊆ S + E exists exactly when S ≠ ⊥ or U is finite. For a submonoid target T, the criterion is S ≠ ⊥ ∨ T = ⊥, without requiring S ≤ T; these are covers, not equalities. If S ≤ T and R : Type u is any commutative semiring, the canonical left inclusion module between the coefficient-one monomial adjoins for S and T in AddMonoidAlgebra R ℕ is finite exactly when Subsingleton R ∨ S ≠ ⊥ ∨ T = ⊥. Thus zero coefficients are included, without a ring, domain, Noetherian, finite-generation or extra positive-generator hypothesis. See NatFiniteRemainder and NatMonomialFinite.
  • Finite relative-root extensions and normalization. For a finitely generated S : AddSubmonoid (σ →₀ ℕ), there is a finite set of relative-root exponents E such that every exponent satisfying (∃ b ∈ S, b + n ∈ S) ∧ ∃ k > 0, k • n ∈ S is s + e with s ∈ S and e ∈ E. The corresponding coefficient-one monomial adjoin is a finite module over the original S-monomial adjoin, over every commutative semiring, for the canonical inclusion action. For an integrally closed commutative domain of coefficients, integralClosure A (FractionRing A) is finite over its own A. Variables and coefficients have independent universes; variables may be infinite, and there is no coefficient-Noetherian assumption. Both relative exponent witnesses are retained; this is not ambient saturation. See FiniteRemainder and MvPolynomial/MonomialFinite.lean.
  • Normalization image of multivariate monomial subalgebras. Let R : Type u be an integrally closed commutative domain, σ : Type v arbitrary, and S : AddSubmonoid (σ →₀ ℕ). Let A be the R-adjoin of the monomials monomial s 1 for s ∈ S. Under the canonical map from Frac(A) to Frac(MvPolynomial σ R), the image of integralClosure A (Frac(A)) is the image of the R-adjoin of the monomials monomial n 1 satisfying (∃ b ∈ S, b + n ∈ S) ∧ ∃ k > 0, k • n ∈ S. The equality is between R-subalgebras, restricting scalars on the integral closure first. This is normalization in A's own fraction field, not ambient integral closure or ambient saturation; even exponents remain even. There are no finiteness, nonempty-variable, field, Noetherian or characteristic assumptions, and the universes are independent. See MvPolynomial/MonomialNormalization.lean.
  • Finite gcd monomial extensions. For an arbitrary additive submonoid S of ℕ and a commutative semiring R, the subalgebra generated by powers of X with exponents in multiples (Nat.setGcd S) is finite over the exponent subalgebra for S, through their canonical inclusion. Over commutative rings the same extension is integral. Gcd zero gives constants; no coefficient-domain, Noetherian, finite-generation or positive-gcd hypothesis is required. See MonomialFinite.
  • Fine components of integral polynomials. Over any commutative ring R, an arbitrary additive submonoid S of σ →₀ ℕ defines the subalgebra generated by monomial s 1 for s ∈ S. If f is integral over this subalgebra, then each monomial n (f.coeff n) is integral over it. An integral monomial with non-nilpotent coefficient has k • n ∈ S for some k > 0; over a reduced ring this applies to every exponent in the support of f. Variables and coefficients have independent universes, with no finiteness or characteristic restriction. These are powers of individual monomials, not a claim that some power of f lies in the subalgebra. See MvPolynomial/MonomialIntegralClosure.lean.
  • Intrinsic normality of multivariate monomial subalgebras. Let R : Type u be any commutative domain, σ : Type v any independently universed variable type, and S any additive submonoid of σ →₀ ℕ. The R-subalgebra generated by monomial s 1 for s ∈ S is integrally closed in its own fraction field if and only if R is integrally closed and b ∈ S, b + n ∈ S, 0 < k and k • n ∈ S imply n ∈ S. Coefficient normality is an output of necessity, not an initial hypothesis. There is no finite-variable, finite-generation, field, Noetherian or characteristic hypothesis. The difference conditions distinguish this relative root closure from ambient saturation; even-exponent subalgebras need not contain the variables. See MvPolynomial/MonomialIntegralClosure.lean.   • Arithmetic gcd recognition. For every additive submonoid S of ℕ, there is a unique d : ℕ such that every element of S is divisible by d and only finitely many multiples of d are missing from S. This d is the canonical Nat.setGcd (S : Set ℕ). The zero submonoid has d = 0; no finite-generation, positivity or coprimality hypothesis is needed. The gaps are relative to the multiples of d, not the full complement of S. See NumberTheory/FrobeniusNumber.lean.
  • Integral closedness forces normal coefficients. Over a commutative domain R, let S be any additive submonoid of ℕ. If the subalgebra generated by X ^ s for s ∈ S is integrally closed in its own fraction field, then R is integrally closed. No base-normality input or positive exponent is needed; the bottom and top submonoids are included. The proof restricts evaluation at zero to an R-linear scalar retraction. See Polynomial/MonomialIntegralClosure.lean.

  • Integral closedness of polynomial exponent subalgebras. Over an integrally closed commutative domain R, the subalgebra generated by X ^ s for an arbitrary additive submonoid S of ℕ is integrally closed if and only if S = AddSubmonoid.multiples d for some d : ℕ. Degree zero gives the constants; positive degree gives an algebra isomorphic to R[X]. The necessity direction holds over every coefficient domain, without base normality. No field, finite-generation, Noetherian or characteristic hypothesis is needed. See Polynomial/MonomialIntegralClosure.lean.

    Normality is absolute, in the subalgebra's own fraction ring. For example, ℤ[X²] is normal but proper in ℤ[X]: X is absent, although it is integral over ℤ[X²] by the monic equation Y² - X². Normality therefore does not mean integral closedness inside the ambient polynomial ring.

  • Monomial gaps obstruct integral closedness. Over any commutative domain R, let S be an additive submonoid of ℕ. If the subalgebra generated by X ^ s for s ∈ S is integrally closed, then b ∈ S, b + n ∈ S, 0 < k and k * n ∈ S imply n ∈ S. The contrapositive shows that R[X², X³] is not integrally closed, in every characteristic. See Polynomial/MonomialIntegralClosure.lean.
  • Power-substitution range and equivalence. Over any commutative semiring R, the range of Polynomial.expand R d is the existing exponent subalgebra for AddSubmonoid.multiples d, including d = 0.
