• Characteristic-power ideals. For commutative rings K and A with a K-bialgebra structure, ExpChar K p and r : ℕ, the ideal generated by all p ^ r-th powers of a coideal ideal is again a coideal. For a given full Hopf structure, using its own bialgebra parent, a Hopf ideal remains a Hopf ideal. The canonical counit kernel gives bialgebra and Hopf quotients on the same ordinary ideal quotient. See HopfAlgebra/Ideal.

Group objects

  • Normality detected on finitely generated algebras. Over any commutative ring K, a given group-scheme monomorphism i : N ⟶ G is normal exactly when the range of N(R) → G(R) is normal for every finitely generated K-algebra R. Only the structure map of G ×_K N is assumed locally of finite type; neither group scheme is required to be affine. Criterion.
  • Flatness of group-homomorphism families. For group objects G and H over any scheme S, let f : G ⟶ H be a group homomorphism and i : P ⟶ G a family. If P ⟶ S is flat and surjective, G ⟶ S is flat, and i ≫ f is flat, then f is flat. Neither a group structure on P nor flatness of i or H ⟶ S is required. See Flat.of_group_hom_family.
  • Family translations. For a given group object G in any Cartesian monoidal category and any family i : P → G, the shears (p, g) ↦ (p, i(p)g) and (g, p) ↦ (gi(p), p) are isomorphisms of underlying objects. No group structure on P or multiplicativity of i is required. Projection and generalized-pair formulas, inverse/unit/composition laws and naturality preserve the noncommutative multiplication order. Construction and factorization.
  • Ordered commutator word maps. For a given group object in any Cartesian monoidal category, a supplied fan indexed by Fin n × Fin 2 determines the ordered product of its n commutators. The API gives evaluation on arbitrary generalized points, the empty and single-pair formulas, naturality under monoid morphisms, and invariance under appending a unit pair. A limiting target fan supplies the extension and gives inclusion of generalized-point ranges. For any supplied limiting fans with n + 1 pairs, a generalized point lies in the ordinary commutator subgroup exactly when it is a value of one of these words. No compatibility between fans or subgroup-image assertion for an individual word map is required. Construction and completeness.
  • Conjugacy of stabilizers. For a given internal group action in a Cartesian monoidal category, an arbitrary group section g induces ModObj.stabilizerConjIso x g between the original chosen stabilizers of x and g • x. Only these two pullbacks are required. The inclusion squares, inverse, all-test conjugation formulas and precomposition naturality retain the original action. Over a scheme, both orbit-fibre projections and all test schemes are retained, with no reducedness or other geometric hypothesis. Internal construction, scheme specialization.
  • Group structure on a monoid kernel. In any Cartesian monoidal category, let G be a group object, H and N monoid objects, and f : G → H and i : N → G monoid morphisms. A supplied pullback of f along the unit of H with legs i and N → 𝟙 gives GrpObj.ofIsPullback f i hPB : GrpObj N, retaining exactly the supplied monoid structure. Its characteristic API gives that equality, the original projection as a group-object morphism, its underlying map, and compatibility with inversion. This constructs kernel group structures without a group target or global pullbacks; the integer-product example has a proper nontrivial kernel and a non-group target. Construction.
  • Inversion criterion. For a group object in a Cartesian monoidal category, commutativity is equivalent to inversion being a monoid morphism. The braiding is explicit and unique in this setting. This is a categorical extension of the inversion criterion used in Milne, Abelian Varieties, proof of Corollary 1.4. Criterion, integer and permutation models.
  • Trivial-kernel criterion. A morphism from a group object to a monoid object in any Cartesian monoidal category is mono exactly when the canonical unit-to-fibre pullback lift is an isomorphism. The supplied fibre needs no group structure or global finite-limit hypothesis. For schemes this is the scheme-theoretic fibre, not a geometric-point test. Criterion, proper S₃ → S₃ × C₂ inclusion and monoid boundary.
  • Kernel pairs of group-to-monoid morphisms. In a Cartesian monoidal category, let G be a group object, Q a monoid object and q : G → Q a monoid morphism. For any kernel pullback N → G over the unit of Q, the kernel-pair statement identifies N × G with G ×_Q G by (n, g) ↦ (i(n)g, g). No group structure on N, inverse operation on Q or global finite-limit hypothesis is required. Statement. GrpObj.isPullback_kernel_mul uses this supplied N and i without a monoid structure on N or a multiplicativity condition on i.

Coalgebras and Hopf algebras

  • Frobenius scalar extension and relative Frobenius. Over a commutative semiring K with ExpChar K p, the coefficient-first Frobenius twist of a semiring K-algebra has a balanced lift into any central semiring K-algebra, characterized by its values on the original factor. For a commutative K-bialgebra A, relative Frobenius is a bialgebra map with value c ⊗ a ↦ algebraMap K A c * a ^ (p ^ r). Its image is the K-scalar span of these powers, which need not be the raw set of powers over an imperfect field. No flatness, finiteness or perfectness is required. Construction and API, scalar roles and universal property.
  • Pure-power coactions over the relative Frobenius image. Over a field K with (p : K) = 0 and prime p, a specified right coaction of a commutative Hopf K-algebra A induces a unique coaction on the original span of (ι v)^(p^r) with coefficients in the full Hopf range of relative Frobenius. Range inclusion recovers the original subtype coaction. The finite-sum generator formula corestricts each coefficient by relative Frobenius, and in particular sends a weight a to a coefficient with ambient value a^(p^r). No perfection, basis or finite-dimensionality hypothesis is needed. Construction and characteristic API.
  • A finitely presented rank-one nonflat coalgebra over a PID. For any field K, put R = K[X], T = R/(X²) and give C = R ⊕ T (implemented as TrivSqZeroExt R T) its primitive R-coalgebra structure. Then C is finitely presented of cardinal module rank one, has the nonzero torsion element (0, [1]) killed by nonzero X², and is not R-flat. Its R-group-like elements are equivalent to K, with parameter k represented by (1, [Polynomial.C k * X]). They are infinite if K is infinite; finite fields are included in the equivalence. This extends the rational construction in Finite generation and group-like elements, §3. Module results, group-like classification. More generally, finite presentation and cardinal rank one hold for any commutative coefficient ring, with Mathlib's rank convention even for the zero ring. Nonzero torsion and nonflatness require only nontriviality; the group-like family is injective over any commutative ring, and it is exhaustive over a reduced ring. Infinitude is deduced only under Infinite K.
  • Scalar extension of specified right-comodule morphisms. For commutative semirings R and S with a given R-algebra structure on S, every colinear map f : X → Y extends between the scalar-extended comodules, with underlying linear map f.toLinearMap.baseChange S. The API gives its value on every tensor and on pure tensors, and preserves identities, composition and any specified split retraction. The coalgebra and module universes are independent; no flatness, projectivity or finiteness is required. Construction and API.
  • Cofree specified right comodules. For an arbitrary coalgebra C over a commutative semiring R and arbitrary R-modules in independent universes, V ⊗[R] C has its canonical cofree coaction. Colinear maps into it correspond R-linearly to ordinary linear maps into V, with both inverse laws, naturalities and triangles. Every specified coaction gives a colinear injective map into this cofree comodule, with a linear retraction; colinearity of that projection is not asserted. At V = R the left unitor recovers the regular-target coefficient correspondence. API, infinite natural-number diagonal use with proper range.
  • Coalgebra data on a linear range. Over a commutative ring, let the source and codomain of a linear map be modules with additive commutative groups. If the source has weak coalgebra data and the kernel is a coideal, the linear range carries the quotient-transported data. The range restriction preserves counit and comultiplication; no coalgebra data on the codomain or full coalgebra laws on the source are required. Construction and preservation.
  • Coideal annihilators and convolution subalgebras. Over a commutative ring, the ordinary full-dual annihilator of a coideal contains the counit and is closed under convolution. These closure results require only CoalgebraStruct on an additive-group module, in independent universes; no coalgebra laws, projectivity, flatness or finite-dimensionality hypothesis is needed. With full Coalgebra laws, the annihilator defines a canonical subalgebra of WithConv (Module.Dual R C), with membership and underlying-submodule API. Module.
  • Coideals, cores and the complete lattice. Over a commutative ring, an additive commutative group with a module structure and CoalgebraStruct, coideals bundle Submodule.IsCoideal and are ordered by inclusion. The greatest coideal contained in a submodule is right adjoint to inclusion; their Galois coinsertion induces a complete lattice. Joins and bottom have ambient submodule carriers; infima and top are cores, not generally ambient intersections or top. The rational natural-number-indexed group-like coalgebra has a nonzero proper counit kernel and a nonzero proper top-coideal carrier. Module, example.
  • Coideal scalar extension. For commutative-ring algebras, extension of scalars preserves coideals; faithfully flat extension reflects them. Only CoalgebraStruct on the additive-group source module is needed, in independent universes: no field, finiteness, coalgebra laws or source/quotient-flatness. The canonical tensor datum and image carrier are Mathlib's; its quotient-based coideal predicate determines the ring/group boundary. Module, one polynomial augmentation model over ℚ → ℚ[X] on AddMonoidAlgebra ℚ ℤ, with nonzero proper carriers and a nonzero X ⊗ (e₀ - e₁) witness.
  • Automatic counit preservation. Over a commutative semiring, a unital algebra map (AlgHom) from a Hopf algebra to a bialgebra that preserves comultiplication automatically preserves the counit. The algebras need not be commutative. Module.
  • Weak linear coefficient composition. Over a commutative semiring R, let C and M be R-semimodules and let Δ : C →ₗ[R] C ⊗[R] C and δ : M →ₗ[R] C ⊗[R] M satisfy relative coassociativity. Arbitrary linear coefficients α β : C →ₗ[R] A, for a possibly noncommutative semiring R-algebra A, induce A-linear operators on A ⊗[R] M. Applying α first and β second combines coefficients as (LinearMap.mul' R A).comp ((TensorProduct.map α β).comp Δ): the outer coefficient α multiplies the inner coefficient β in that order. No multiplication on C, counit or multiplicativity of the coefficients is needed. Composition theorem; the matrix-unit example distinguishes the two orders.
  • Regular coefficient normalization and recovery. Over a commutative semiring R, for arbitrary R-modules M,N and a linear map F : M →ₗ[R] R ⊗[R] N, conjugating its tensor extension by the left unitors gives lid ∘ F. This module-only normalization API specializes to F = (TensorProduct.map ell id) ∘ Δ, giving lid ∘ ell.rTensor C ∘ Δ for arbitrary Δ : C →ₗ[R] C ⊗[R] C and ell : C →ₗ[R] R. If C is a coalgebra and Δ its registered comultiplication, postcomposition with the counit recovers ell. All scalar and module universes are independent; no multiplication on C, field, finiteness, flatness or projectivity is required. Coefficient API.
  • Ordered rational matrix-point operators. For arbitrary U,V ∈ GL₂(ℚ) with (UV)₀₀ = 2 and (VU)₀₀ = 1, the two raw tensor-extended coefficient operator compositions differ. The points are the general-linear coordinate ring's multiplicative-equivalence images, and a supplied linear map is given equal to its registered comultiplication. The registered-counit readbacks, retaining both left unitors, are respectively 2 and 1: applying β after α corresponds to UV, not VU. Operators and readbacks.
  • Scalar-extension characteristics. Over a commutative semiring R, for any semiring R-algebra A and R-semimodule M, the tensor extension of a linear map F : M →ₗ[R] A ⊗[R] M is the identity exactly when F(m) = 1 ⊗ₜ[R] m. For a semiring R-algebra C, arbitrary linear Δ : C →ₗ[R] C ⊗[R] C and given δ : M →ₗ[R] C ⊗[R] M, relative coassociativity is equivalent to composition of the tensor-square operators for the ordered left and right inclusions. No commutativity of A or C, coalgebra structure or law on Δ is required. Identity characteristic, tensor-square characteristic.
  • Canonical-point counit criterion. For an R-bialgebra C over a commutative semiring R and an arbitrary linear map δ : M →ₗ[R] C ⊗[R] M, the counit law holds exactly when the tensor-extended operator at the convolution unit is the identity for every commutative coefficient algebra in Type (max uR uC w). The module universe is independent of this tester universe; no coaction laws, flatness or injectivity of scalar extension are assumed. The bialgebra C need not be commutative, so the commutative-coordinate case follows by specialization. Criterion, zero-map boundary test.
  • Canonical-point coassociativity and coaction-law criteria. When the coordinate bialgebra C is commutative, a given left linear datum δ is coassociative exactly when applying the operator for α and then that for β gives the operator at the convolution product α ⋆ β, for all algebra points into every commutative coefficient algebra in Type (max uR uC w). No counit law or bound on the module universe is required. The combined criterion pairs this composition law with the unit law, characterizing both coaction laws together. Criteria, non-coassociative boundary test.
  • Scalar-extension postcomposition. Over a commutative semiring R, extending an R-linear map F : M →ₗ[R] N to an A-linear map A ⊗[R] M →ₗ[A] N commutes with A-linear postcomposition when the target modules have compatible R- and A-actions. The scalar R-algebra A may be a noncommutative semiring; for commutative A the extension agrees with LinearMap.liftBaseChange. API.
  • Unit-counit normalization. For a commutative semiring R and an additive-commutative-monoid R-module C carrying a full Coalgebra R C, an element with tensor-square comultiplication and unit counit is group-like. Scalar and carrier universes are independent; no multiplication on C is needed. Module.
  • Group-like elements and scalar extension. The counit-one and tensor-square equations are preserved by extension of commutative semirings and reflected by faithfully flat extension of commutative rings, requiring only weak coalgebra data. The full group-like predicate has corresponding corollaries, with no flatness or finiteness assumption on the coalgebra module. Module.
  • Group-like elements of tensor products. For full coalgebras over an arbitrary commutative semiring, normalized group-like elements of the tensor product correspond canonically to ordered pairs of group-like elements. The equivalence uses the two counit projections, has pure-tensor inverse values, and is natural under coalgebra morphisms. For bialgebras it is multiplicative, without requiring commutativity of the two bialgebras or nontriviality of the scalar semiring. Coalgebra equivalence, multiplicative equivalence.
  • Tensor products of coalgebra morphisms. For a commutative R-algebra S, an S-linear coalgebra map and an R-linear coalgebra map induce an S-linear map on tensor products over R. Only CoalgebraStruct on independently universed additive-monoid modules is needed; identity and composition are respected, with a bridge to the full-law tensor map. Module.
  • Tensor products of coalgebra quotients. For coideals in modules with weak coalgebra structures over a commutative ring, the sum of the two tensor relation images is a coideal. Mathlib's linear comparison refines to a coalgebra equivalence on the canonical quotients, with representative formulas, a quotient-map square and unique factorization of maps vanishing on the relation sum. No flatness or coalgebra-law hypothesis is needed. Module, integer product example.
  • Coalgebra morphism range constructions. Over a field, CoalgHom.range equips exactly the underlying linear-map range with the restricted coalgebra; CoalgHom.rangeRestrict uses codomain restriction to that carrier. The source needs only AddCommMonoid, Module and CoalgebraStruct; the target has an additive commutative group and a full coalgebra. No source coalgebra laws or finite-dimensionality assumptions are used. The characteristic membership, evaluation and unique-factorization laws are stated alongside the constructions. Module, infinite monoid-algebra example.
  • Tensor products of subcoalgebras. Over a field, the range of the tensor product of two subcoalgebra inclusions is spanned by the pure tensors from their factors. Membership is characterized by an arbitrary tensor preimage; containment in any submodule is equivalent to containment of those generators. Module.
  • Surjective descent of coalgebra laws. Coassociativity and both counitality laws descend over a commutative semiring from a coalgebra to a fixed CoalgebraStruct along a surjective CoalgHomClass map. Coalgebra.ofSurjective equips exactly that target structure with the laws; its parent, comultiplication and counit are unchanged, and it agrees with an existing full target structure. For a linear equivalence e : D ≃ₗ[R] C, LinearEquiv.toCoalgEquiv identifies the weak pullback data with the given data on C. From only e and a coalgebra on C, Coalgebra.ofSurjective_coalgebra uses its inverse morphism to recover exactly e.coalgebra R. Cocommutativity descends to the constructed coalgebra. The infinite semiring projection model uses the constructor; the nonsurjective zero-source boundary explains why surjectivity is needed. The negation example on ℤ distinguishes the pulled-back counit from the scalar counit on the same module. Module, semiring example.
  • Coideal quotient morphisms. Over a commutative ring, coalgebra morphisms vanishing on a coideal descend uniquely; maps preserving coideals induce quotient maps respecting identity and composition. Additive-group modules with CoalgebraStruct suffice, in independent universes; no coalgebra laws, flatness, projectivity or field hypothesis. Module.
  • Bialgebra quotient factorization. Over a commutative ring R, let A be a possibly noncommutative ring and B a semiring, both full R-bialgebras. A bialgebra morphism f : A →ₐc[R] B factors canonically and uniquely through A ⧸ I for a two-sided ideal I contained in its ordinary ring kernel, with I.restrictScalars R a coideal. This uses Mathlib's fixed canonical quotient bialgebra and asserts uniqueness of the factor, not of quotient structures. Module.
  • Kernels with a flat quotient. Over a commutative ring, the kernel of a linear map preserving comultiplication and counit is a coideal when the target module and the ordinary module quotient by the kernel are flat. No surjectivity or coalgebra laws are required. Module.
  • Coideal generator criterion. Over a commutative ring, the span of an arbitrary set in an additive-group module with CoalgebraStruct is a coideal iff the counit and (q ⊗ q) ∘ Δ vanish on every generator, where q is the ordinary module quotient map by that span. Scalar and module universes are independent; no coalgebra laws, field, flatness, finiteness or nonemptiness are required. Module.
  • Surjective kernels. Over a commutative ring, the linear kernel of a surjective coalgebra morphism is a coideal. Independent-universe additive-group modules with CoalgebraStruct suffice; no coalgebra laws or flatness assumption. Module.
  • Surjective first isomorphism. Over a commutative ring, a surjective coalgebra morphism identifies its target with the quotient by its linear kernel, with the canonical quotient structure. Both independent-universe additive-group modules need only CoalgebraStruct; no coalgebra laws or flatness. Representative, inverse and linear-equivalence formulas characterize the map. Module.
  • Coideal correspondence. For a surjective coalgebra morphism over a commutative ring, image and preimage give an order isomorphism between source coideals containing its linear kernel and target coideals. Only CoalgebraStruct is required; no coalgebra laws, flatness, field or finiteness assumption. Module.
  • Kernels over a field. The linear kernel of any coalgebra morphism is a coideal, without surjectivity. Both source and target need only CoalgebraStruct on additive-group modules, in independent universes; no coalgebra laws or finite-dimensionality hypothesis is required. The field makes the quotient by the kernel flat; flatness of the ambient modules alone is not sufficient over general commutative rings. Module, noninjective, nonsurjective example.
  • Finite subcoalgebras. Over a field, every finitely generated subspace or finite subset of a coalgebra lies in a finite-dimensional subcoalgebra, with restricted coalgebra laws, bundled inclusion and unique morphism factorization. No ambient finite dimension, algebra/Hopf structure or least finite closure is assumed. Modules and example.
  • Subcoalgebras with a group-like basis. Over a field, a given group-like basis b identifies subsets of its index type with subcoalgebras through b.groupLikeSubcoalgebraOrderIso. The API gives the span of the chosen basis vectors, membership by finite coordinate support, inverse basis membership, and extensionality on basis vectors. Arbitrary and empty index types are allowed, including the zero coalgebra with its empty basis. For the unit coordinate basis, the classification agrees with supported diagonal subcoalgebras. No ambient finite dimension is assumed. Classification module.
  • Supported diagonal subcoalgebras. Over a field, for any index type and set s, Finsupp.supportedSubcoalgebra K s consists exactly of the finitely supported functions whose support lies in s, equivalently the span of the unit coordinate vectors indexed by s. Its API gives membership, span, coordinate inclusion and carrier-containment criteria. Finite s gives a finitely generated carrier and a module-finite subcoalgebra; neither the index type nor s need be finite for the construction. Module, diagonal examples.
  • Subcoalgebra images. Over a field, injectivity of a coalgebra map only on a subcoalgebra gives an equivalence with its image, preserving and reflecting module-finiteness and finite generation. Ambient equivalences transport these carriers as an ordinary Equiv, not an order isomorphism; no ambient finiteness. Module.
  • Algebra-valued tests for right-comodule stability. Let R and C be commutative semirings, with C an R-algebra and a coalgebra on its canonical R-module, and let X be a specified right coaction on any additive-monoid R-module M. A submodule U is image-stable exactly when identity evaluation preserves U.baseChange C, the image of the tensor inclusion. Equivalently, every algebra-valued evaluation preserves U.baseChange A, for commutative R-algebras A in the compatible maximum of the coalgebra and tester universes. The forward implication permits an independently sized A. Evaluation is the stock scalar-extension lift of the commuted coaction; no flatness, bialgebra structure, finiteness, basis or tensor-inclusion injectivity is required. Criteria and API.
  • Linear-coefficient evaluation of right coactions. For a coalgebra C over a commutative semiring K, a specified right coaction on V and any linear map φ : C →ₗ[K] A to a commutative K-algebra A define an A-linear endomorphism of A ⊗[K] V. Evaluation is natural in the scalar algebra and colinear maps; the counit gives the identity, and every image-stable submodule has invariant scalar-extension image. No algebra structure on C, flatness or finiteness is required. Algebra-homomorphism inputs recover the stability criteria above. For the same right coaction, convolution satisfies T_(φ * ψ) = T_φ.comp T_ψ: the operator for ψ acts first. A Hopf algebra gives a homomorphism from algebra-valued convolution points to the units of the scalar-extended endomorphism monoid. Its inverse evaluates after the source antipode; coefficient changes and colinear maps intertwine these operators. Stable submodules retain their original tensor-inclusion images, preserved in both directions by the invertible point operators. Data and API, composition, point actions.
  • Convolution groups and algebra-valued points. Algebra maps from a Hopf algebra over a commutative semiring to any commutative algebra form a group under convolution, without a coalgebra structure on the target. The named group structure uses the existing convolution monoid and agrees with the full original group structure when the target is also a bialgebra. For commutative Hopf algebras over commutative rings, postcomposition defines a group-valued functor on commutative algebras in independent universes. The same-universe spectrum comparison preserves the antipode inverse and change of coefficients. Convolution API, point functor.
  • Changing the coacting coalgebra. Over a commutative semiring, a coalgebra morphism f : C →ₗc[R] D sends a specified right coaction ρ on V to (id_V ⊗ f)ρ on the same module. Full coalgebras and additive-monoid modules in independent universes suffice; no finiteness, flatness, projectivity or injectivity hypothesis is needed. The operation preserves identity, composition and colinear maps; change along the counit gives the scalar coaction for every comodule. API and use.   • Inverses of colinear linear equivalences. For specified right comodules X and Y over the same full coalgebra, a colinear linear equivalence e : M ≃ₗ[R] N has colinear inverse, by Coalgebra.RightComoduleHom.colinear_symm. This works over a commutative semiring on additive-commutative-monoid modules in independent universes, without flatness, a basis or finiteness.   • Kernels of right-comodule morphisms. Over a commutative ring, flatness of the coacting coalgebra alone gives the ordinary linear kernel its induced right coaction. Every comodule morphism annihilated by the given morphism factors uniquely through the kernel, with ordinary linear codomain restriction as the underlying lift. No flatness of either ambient module is required, and all universes are independent. Kernel and lifts.
  • Image stability of right-comodule morphisms. Over a commutative semiring, the linear range of a morphism between specified right comodules is image-stable, without flatness of the coacting coalgebra or either module. Ranges.
  • Canonical range factorization. When the coacting coalgebra alone is flat, the same linear range has its induced right coaction. Its canonical linear range restriction is a surjective comodule morphism, with inclusion factorization and uniqueness. No flatness of the source or target module is required. Construction.
  • Finite right subcomodules. Over a commutative semiring, for additive-monoid modules, projectivity of the coacting coalgebra alone gives finite-subset containment in finitely generated subcomodules. Neither module need be finite, nor the coacted-on module projective. Flatness of the coacting coalgebra suffices for unique subtype coactions from image stability and unique colinear factorization; the counit need not be onto. Module. With projectivity of the coacting coalgebra, the module-finite subcomodules form a nonempty directed covering system containing every finite subset. Every compatible family of colinear maps from these specified stages to any target comodule glues to a unique colinear map on the original module. Restriction and gluing are mutually inverse data and commute with target postcomposition. Over a field these are the finite-dimensional stages, with no flatness, projectivity or finiteness hypothesis on the coalgebra or ambient vector space. More generally, directed covering sets of subcomodules have the same gluing property when the coacting coalgebra is flat. Gluing and readbacks. With flatness of the coacting coalgebra, containment of specified subcomodules gives canonical colinear inclusions with the ordinary submodule inclusion as underlying map. A nonzero coordinate line in the regular ℕ[ℤ] comodule embeds non-surjectively in the proper stable finitely generated span of two coordinates. Inclusion model.
  • The additive group's polynomial regular comodule. Over any commutative ring K, transport of the regular comodule to K[X] keeps the coacting coalgebra SymmetricAlgebra K K fixed. Its generator has coaction X ⊗ 1 + 1 ⊗ t; the binomial formula proves stability of the usual degree-at-most-n submodules. These are module-finite subcomodules, increase and cover every polynomial, with finrank n + 1 over every nontrivial commutative ring and finite-dimensional stages over a field. Over a nontrivial base every stage is proper and nonzero, while the ambient module is not module-finite. This is a comodule model, not an assertion of a polynomial Hopf-algebra equivalence or the full representation correspondence. Construction and API.
  • Ground-field invariance is not all-algebra invariance. In this same polynomial right comodule over 𝔽₂, the nonzero proper line spanned by X² + X is fixed by every ground-field point on the entire tensor-inclusion image. Over 𝔽₂[ε]/(ε²), the point ε sends its generator to 1 ⊗ (X² + X) + ε ⊗ 1, outside the original inclusion image. The scalar-extended constant-coefficient linear map annihilates that image and takes the translated vector to the nonzero element ε. Thus this is failure of invariance for the same coaction and original image, not merely a comparison of different subspace predicates. Countermodel.
  • Natural representations and specified right comodules. For a commutative Hopf algebra C over a commutative ring K and any K-module V, PointRepresentation.rightComoduleEquiv constructs the correspondence between natural point representations on R ⊗[K] V and specified right coactions on the same V. Their scalar extensions give Mathlib's bundled intertwining maps. No finiteness, basis or flatness is needed. For infinite V this is the tensor-extension functor, not Spec Sym(V*).   • Invariant original submodules and unique restriction. Invariance means that all algebra-valued point operators preserve the image of the original scalar-extended subtype inclusion, on every tensor in that image. It is equivalent to image stability for the same recovered coaction, without flatness. If C is flat over K, the restricted coaction is constructed on exactly the original subtype U; its inclusion equation uniquely determines its coaction map and specified comodule value. The corresponding restricted point representation is likewise uniquely determined by the original inclusion as a point intertwiner. Component and arbitrary-algebra squares use precisely U.subtype.baseChange R. No injectivity is assumed over a general coefficient algebra; over a field it follows from flatness of vector spaces.   • Changes of test-algebra universe. Canonical extension presents the same recovered coaction at another tester level. Stock-lift restriction is constructed separately from the given representation's components at lifted coefficient algebras, followed by general linear coefficient descent. Its equality with canonical change, both inverse laws, iterated-lift and common-maximum comparisons are equations of representation values. Tensor comparisons are base-linear and coefficient-semilinear; conjugation and every-algebra-map descent retain the same coaction and point operators. Intertwiner transport retains the original linear map and its scalar extensions, respecting identity and composition. Universe and Hom coherence.
  • The whole finite-dimensional subrepresentation system. Over a field, finite stages are the module-finite subcomodules of the recovered coaction, with their original subspace subtypes and restricted representations. They contain zero, are directed under finite sums, cover V and contain every finite subset; no ambient or coordinate-algebra finite dimension is assumed. Canonical transition and ambient point maps retain the ordinary submodule inclusions and satisfy identity, composition and the ambient triangle. Empty indices and zero rings are allowed; no group/Hopf isomorphism is asserted. Module.
  • Closed unitriangular stages. Over any commutative ring, native finite stage quotients are Hopf algebras with closed stage/successor group-scheme arrows and multiplicative points over every coefficient algebra. Their polynomial coordinates identify the underlying schemes with affine spaces over the base, not additive groups or Hopf algebras; zero rings, empty ranks and exhausted stages are included. Modules and guides.
  • Stage normality, coordinates and kernel. All stages are normal subgroup objects with conjugation and matrix-point readbacks. For 1 ≤ r, primitive superdiagonal coordinates define S_r → Gₐ^J, with successor its underlying fiber over the unit. The entire underlying arrow is an affine-space projection; setting higher variables to zero sections that arrow, not the group map. Zero rings and empty indices are allowed; no quotient or additive splitting. Modules and guides.
  • Unitriangular base change. For arbitrary same-universe commutative rings R → S and finite linearly ordered indices, scalar extension of the actual GL quotient is the quotient over S. The comparison preserves the independently given coproduct, counit and antipode and induces an isomorphism of the actual pulled-back group object. Identity and scalar-tower laws agree with tensor unit/cancellation and canonical pullback compositors (direct to iterated). Zero rings and empty indices are included, without flatness or injectivity. Algebra modules, scheme comparison, scheme coherence.
  • Triangular geometry and dimension. Over any commutative ring, native multiplicative, finite diagonal and finite upper-triangular schemes are smooth with geometrically integral fibers; total spaces are integral over a domain. Empty indices and zero rings are included in the relative results. Over every field the actual diagonal and upper-triangular ring/scheme dimensions are N and N + N.choose 2. No arbitrary-base or general product-dimension formula. Modules, retained indices and coordinate readbacks.
  • Coordinate elimination. Over any commutative ring, eliminating polynomial coordinates commutes with localization, even if the erased denominator is zero. Over a field with finitely many variables, a supplied field-valued point not annihilating the denominator gives full localization dimension; nonzero alone need not supply that point over a finite field. This is a limitation of the supplied-point hypothesis, not of the general nonzero-denominator formula. Elimination, dimension.
  • Represented triangular products. Over any commutative ring, for finite same-universe linearly ordered indices, U ⊗ D ≅ T as schemes over Spec K. The forward map is i(u)e(d); the inverse uses diagonal projection and column normalization. Conjugation on the actual identity fiber is dᵢ uᵢⱼ dⱼ⁻¹, and multiplication is (u,d)(u',d') = (u α(d,u'), dd'), not a direct-product group law. Readbacks allow all coefficient algebras, zero/nonreduced rings, empty indices and noninjective base maps. The diagonal projection splits with scheme-theoretic fiber U; for finite decidable same-universe indices, D is a product of Gₘ, including the empty terminal case. No global transported group instance, arbitrary-base change or normality of U in GL is asserted. Modules and limits.
  • Unitriangular point-group series. Over a commutative ring, finite native unitriangular groups have central/lower-central series, successive additive quotients, derived series and sharp nilpotency bounds under the stated hypotheses. Prime-characteristic exponent results have separate coefficient and size conditions. Series, exact class.
  • GL and SL dimensions. Over every field and finite decidable indices, the actual coordinate rings and affine schemes have dimensions N² and N² - 1, with natural subtraction before casting; rank zero gives zero in both. Dimension, related geometry.
  • Infinite vector-space dimension of GL coordinates. Over a field and nonempty finite decidable indices, powers of any universal matrix entry are linearly independent, so the coordinate algebra is not module-finite. This is not just carrier infinitude or Krull dimension; empty indices give the field. Module and GL₂ example.
