• First-order conormal maps. For an arbitrary extension P → S of commutative algebras over a commutative ring K, a K-algebra section of P/I² → S makes the canonical conormal map I/I² → S ⊗[P] Ω[P⁄K] injective. If S is formally étale over K, this map is an equivalence. Neither result assumes smoothness of P or a section into P. The equivalence's forward map is Mathlib's cotangentComplex, with an evaluation law on every kernel class.
  • Cotangent spaces and differentials. For a local algebra over a commutative ring, the residue-field equivalence identifies its cotangent space with the residue-field base change of Kähler differentials when the residue field is formally étale over the base; no residue section is needed. It agrees with the split-residue construction when a section is supplied. The latter requires only formal unramifiedness of the residue field, and also applies to a supplied residue-field algebra equivalence with the base. At a maximal ideal of a finite-type algebra over an algebraically closed field, the closed-point comparison applies. Formal étaleness of the residue extension is not automatic.
  • Presentation relations and local Jacobian criteria. A finite presentation admits a linearly independent selection of relations spanning its residue-field cotangent kernel at any prime over a commutative base; dimension and localization provide related comparison tools. To infer formal smoothness of a local target, the relation criterion additionally requires a formally smooth presenting algebra with finite free differentials, a finite family generating the presentation kernel, and independence of its residue-field cotangent images. The quotient criterion derives generation only with a finite-dimensional domain quotient and the stated Krull-dimension comparison; it does not require the target to be a domain.
  • Smooth implies regular. A standard-smooth field-algebra has regular prime localizations. The smooth-at-prime result requires a finite-type algebra over a field, and the smooth-locus result requires a scheme locally of finite type over a field; smooth points have regular local stalks. The general converse, regular implies smooth, is not proved here.
  • Dedekind consequences. Smooth field-algebras are regular, and a regular domain of dimension at most one is Dedekind (including the zero-dimensional field case). Thus a smooth domain over a field with that dimension bound is Dedekind. For schemes, the global-sections theorem requires an integral, affine, smooth scheme over a field of topological dimension at most one; it does not assert the nonaffine, nonintegral or higher-dimensional cases.

The Artinian criteria state that a commutative Artinian local ring is regular if and only if it is a field, and that a commutative Artinian ring is regular if and only if it is reduced, including the zero ring. The proofs use the dimension-zero and localization criteria from Mathlib. See the signature and boundary guide.
